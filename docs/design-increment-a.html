Where this comes from

Every mechanic below is grounded in evidence gathered today: a 105-agent deep-research pass over Tibia's actual mechanics (11 verified findings, down to the server source code that implements attack exhaust), frame-by-frame vision analysis of three Tibia videos (solo hunt, team hunt, team-roles tutorial) through the adapted Foreman pipeline, and a human-biased vision critique of our own captures by a Tibia-veteran persona. Your locked calls: full monster flip · gambit rules · pack of 3 · combat core first.

1 The world frame — The Advancing Nest

Your "between warband and siege" answer, resolved.

One city, falling district by district. The city is a chain of hand-authored districts — our proven zone tech. Your nest is the hub, but the nest moves: when you break a district, you re-nest in its ruins and that becomes your new safe zone. The old districts stay conquered-dark behind you; the frontier ahead is alive with humans.

From the warband idea

The arc of destruction — you advance, the world visibly falls behind you. Progress is territory, not numbers.

From the siege idea

One coherent city, escalating resistance the deeper you push: militia → police → army.

Why this wins on engineering

At any moment it is still hub-and-spoke: one safe zone (current nest), one hunting zone (current frontier). The tech we fun-verified today carries over 1:1 — "advancing" is re-pointing which zone is home. No mega-map, no persistence system, no camera rework for slice one.

Increment A ships the nest + District One as hunting ground. The break-and-advance flow is Increment B.

2 Combat cadence — the exhaust metronome

Kills the held-space barrier exploit. Verified against Tibia's actual server source.

The research settled how Tibia really does it: fixed-interval action windows on separate clocks. Melee is a wall-clock exhaust (lastMeleeAttack timestamp, ~2s beat); healing and attacking run on separate cooldown groups; one meaningful action per window. That scarcity — not APM — is the entire skill ceiling.

Attack lane changed

A swing costs an exhaust window (~45 frames / 0.75s, data-driven). Holding attack no longer spams — it auto-swings on the beat, exactly like Tibia's auto-attack. Between beats you are just a body standing there, and human Shooters punish statues.

swing · exhaust · swing · exhaust · swing — the rhythm you play

Dodge lane unchanged

Separate clock (50f cooldown). Offense never gates survival — the two-lane structure is the deepest thing the team-hunt footage teaches.

Input buffering new

One buffered press per lane. Early input queues and fires on the first legal tick. No "you are exhausted" spam — the window renders as a filling pip on the creature.

The feel layer (hitstop, shake — the part you called fun) fires on the successful beat, making the metronome proprioceptive: you'll feel the window the way Tibia veterans feel exhaust.

3 The pack — Brute / Stalker / Spitter

Tibia's cooperation trinity, monster-side. Locked at 3.

The team-hunt breakdown proves Tibia builds four cooperative jobs out of three rules: bodies block tiles, AoE pays per body stacked, shared exhaust means nobody covers two jobs. Your pack IS that trinity — possessed one at a time, gambits driving the rest. Tab cycles possession; the input seam makes the swap nearly free.

BRUTE

The Brute — blocker EK DNA

Slow, high HP. His body is the wall — parked in a doorway he caps how many humans reach the pack. Cleave arc on the beat; taunt-roar (aggro override — the exeta res of monsters). Holding a choke is an active job: heal-gambit + taunt rotation, not a statue.

STALKER

The Stalker — puller RP DNA

Fast, dodge-heavy, single-target pounce. Walking past humans aggros them — lure trains: sweep a street, snake a squad behind you, collapse them onto the Brute's choke. Owns the pace of the hunt, exactly like the paladin owns Tibia's.

SPITTER

The Spitter — shooter MS DNA

Ranged spit on the attack lane, plus an arcing AoE glob on a long lane (~25s) — the payoff cast. Damage scales with bodies caught, so the pull-compress-detonate loop from the solo footage becomes the pack's signature play.

The loop this creates

Stalker lures a squad
→
Brute holds the choke
→
humans queue & stack
→
Spitter detonates
→
loot sweep, re-lure

Gather ~15s → payoff ~2s → sweep. The exact rhythm both videos measured. You can possess any vertex of the triangle and the other two keep playing their roles.

4 Gambits — programming your pack

FF12-style ordered IF/THEN rules. Locked.

Each creature carries an ordered rule list in JSON. Conditions and actions come from a fixed, registered vocabulary (~10 each — unknown symbol raises, same doctrine as the event bus). First matching rule wins, evaluated on a 1s think tick (Tibia's monster pulse).

{
  "brute": [
    { "if": "hp_below_30",      "then": "retreat_to_nest_side" },
    { "if": "ally_swarmed",     "then": "taunt_roar" },
    { "if": "human_in_choke",   "then": "hold_position" },
    { "if": "human_adjacent",   "then": "attack" },
    { "if": "always",           "then": "guard_spitter" }
  ]
}

In Increment A you edit these as JSON files — you're the dev-tester, and hand-editing proves the vocabulary before we spend an increment on UI. The in-game gambit editor is Increment B, parked.

5 Humans — the farm that fights back

Two archetypes doing all the encounter design. The Falcon lesson.

ArchetypeBehaviorWhy it exists
Rusher Melee. Beelines to adjacency, body-blocks, queues at chokes (cardinal-preferring pathing so corridors matter). Cooperation food — makes the Brute's choke and the Spitter's density payoff work.
Shooter Holds a 3–5 tile standoff and fires on the beat. Won't enter the blob. Formation breaker — the only thing forcing the pack to move. Punishes exhaust-statues.

Squads spawn from doorway pull nodes — humanity keeps sending people. And the flavor-perfect mechanic the research handed us for free: humans flee below 25% HP (Tibia's own runAwayHealth, verified in source). A terrified human sprinting for a doorway is both theme and decision: chase the runner or hold formation?

6 District One

"Terraza Norte" (working name). One hand-authored zone.

The nest hub re-theme

Warm dark organic palette — the town reskinned. Safe. Where you swap gambit files and (later) spend biomass. Death sends the pack home here.

The frontier

Streets + a plaza + interior chokes (a store, an alley). Authored as pathing tooling: corridor mouths, back-walls, lure loops — geometry is the encounter design language.

7 Vision-critique debts shipped with it

Every high-severity finding from the Tibia-veteran critique of our own captures.

  • Facing notch on every creature — "the cheapest, highest-value pixel in the game"
  • Hurt-flash never white (deep red) — player must never resemble the enemy
  • Telegraph → magenta; gold means exits and nothing else
  • Walls 2–3× floor brightness — chokepoints must read at a glance
  • Corpses persist — human corpses become the loot channel later; a kill must change the world
  • Attack lunge/recoil (2px) + swing arc that sweeps and fades — even a rect can throw a punch
  • Ease-out step tween — kill the linear soap-bar glide; the grid should feel like a drumbeat
  • Knockback tweened, never teleported; HP bars over engaged enemies

8 Deliberately rejected

  • Heal-tanking as survival — dodge stays the defensive verb; density is a priced bet
  • Text-log combat feedback — feel carries state; rejection spam replaced by buffering + pips
  • Full scripting language for gambits — tooling tax would eat increments before fun-verify
  • 4th pack member (healer) — folds into a Brute gambit for now
  • In-game gambit editor UI — Increment B, after the vocabulary proves itself
  • District progression / nest advance flow — Increment B; A proves the pack fight
  • UI density arms race — HP, exhaust pip, long-CD arc. Everything else is a pause screen

9 Ship gate

rake green · replay captures byte-identical across runs · vision critique passes on captured frames · you possess all three creatures in a real hunt and call the pack fight fun. Nothing new starts until then.
