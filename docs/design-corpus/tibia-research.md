# Deep research: Tibia combat mechanics (11 verified findings)

Tibia's moment-to-moment feel comes from a slow, legible, rhythmic action economy layered on strict tile occupancy: combat is auto-attack based (engage once, then swings repeat on a fixed ~2s beat with no per-press input), manual actions (spells/runes) are gated by layered exhaust/cooldown groups so healing and attacking run on separate clocks, and special attacks are chance-rolled per interval, producing steady melee punctuated by random damage spikes. Positional play emerges from two hard rules: a defender can block at most 2 incoming melee attacks regardless of skill, and an occupied tile is absolutely impassable to pathfinding — so body-blocking a 1-tile corridor is the canonical tactic, officially endorsed by the game manual. Creature AI is simple, deterministic, and data-driven: independent per-behavior tick accumulators off a 1s think pulse, a pure HP-threshold flee check that flips pathfinding into greedy run-to-viewport-edge mode, and the signature 'monster dance' of random distance-preserving side-steps. Movement feel comes from grid quantization: A* charges diagonals 2.5x cardinal cost, and player speed snaps to discrete breakpoints rather than scaling continuously. For game-two, the design core is: fixed-interval auto-attacks + separate heal/attack cooldown clocks + hard tile-blocking + dumb-but-readable per-timer creature AI.

## [high] Combat is auto-attack based on a fixed interval beat: once a character or creature engages, attacks repeat automatically at regular intervals (baseline ~2000ms for melee), not per button press. The canonical TFS dragon has melee at interval=2000 with no chance gate, so an adjacent blocker takes a guaranteed melee attempt every 2 seconds.
Evidence: Official manual verbatim: 'Attacks take place at regular intervals, i.e. once characters or creatures have started striking at an opponent they will go on striking steadily.' dragon.xml: '<attack name="melee" interval="2000" skill="50" attack="40"/>' with engine default chance=100 (functionally un-gated). Community consensus corroborates a 2s auto-attack cooldown with hits at exact multiples of the interval. Merges claims [0] and [10].
Sources: https://www.tibia.com/gameguides/?subtopic=manual&section=combat; https://github.com/otland/tfs-old-svn/blob/master/data/monster/Dragons/dragon.xml; https://github.com/otland/forgottenserver/blob/master/src/monster.cpp

## [high] TFS implements monster attack cadence as per-spell interval gating with two mechanisms: non-melee spells use a tick accumulator (attackTicks accumulates elapsed time; a spell fires at most once per its per-spell `speed` ms window, modulo check refuses re-use within a round), while melee uses a wall-clock exhaust — an absolute int64_t lastMeleeAttack timestamp checked as (OTSYS_TIME() - lastMeleeAttack) < sb.speed. Fleeing monsters never melee (isFleeing() is in the same guard). This produces the fixed rhythmic beat rather than continuous DPS.
Evidence: Verified verbatim in Monster::canUseSpell on master: non-melee branch 'if (sb.speed > attackTicks) ... if (attackTicks % sb.speed >= interval)'; melee branch 'if (isFleeing() || (OTSYS_TIME() - lastMeleeAttack) < sb.speed) return false'; monster.h declares 'int64_t lastMeleeAttack = 0;'. lastMeleeAttack is set on each swing and reset to 0 when a swing lands out of reach (clearing the cooldown early). Merges claims [3], [4], [8] — [8] was a semantic duplicate of [4]'s mechanism.
Sources: https://github.com/otland/forgottenserver/blob/master/src/monster.cpp; https://github.com/otland/forgottenserver/blob/master/src/monster.h

## [high] Non-melee monster attacks are gated by a per-interval percentage chance rather than firing every cooldown: the dragon's fire missile rolls 15% every 2000ms (range 7, radius 4, -45..-65) and its fire wave rolls 8% every 3000ms (length 8, spread 3, -90..-150). Combined with un-gated melee, the pattern is steady melee punctuated by random damage spikes — the core threat texture of Tibia combat.
Evidence: Both attack lines verified verbatim in dragon.xml; engine cross-check confirms doAttacking rolls 'it->chance >= random_range(1,100)' once per eligible interval round, and chance-less melee defaults to 100. Claim [11].
Sources: https://github.com/otland/tfs-old-svn/blob/master/data/monster/Dragons/dragon.xml

## [medium] Player action economy is a layered exhaust/cooldown system: every spell has an individual cooldown plus a primary group cooldown (Attack / Healing / Support) and possibly a secondary group, with durations differing per spell (e.g. Strong Ice Strike locks itself and its secondary group 8s but the attack group only 2s). Healing and attacking therefore run on separate clocks. Runes have no individual cooldown — using an attack rune only locks its group for 2s (the classic rune-exhaust cadence). The modern cooldown model (Update 8.70, Dec 2010) is an extension of the older Exhaust system; classic 7.4 exhaust was a two-class split: aggressive actions (attacks, damage spells, runes) share a longer cooldown, non-aggressive (healing, utility) a shorter one (community formulas: ~2s / ~1s).
Evidence: Fandom Cooldown page verified verbatim for the layered model, the Strong Ice Strike example, and 'Runes do not have individual cooldowns and only affect their cooldown group for 2 seconds'; official manual corroborates the 3 main spell groups + secondary groups + group cooldowns. Tibiara verified verbatim for the 7.4 aggressive/non-aggressive split (no exact durations on that page; OTLand community formulas supply 2s/1s). CipSoft's 2010 teaser corroborates cooldowns as a revision of the delay/exhaust system. Merges claims [15], [16], [17], [18]. Medium because the load-bearing sources are secondary wikis (though internally consistent and partially corroborated by the official manual), and [16] was a 2-1 vote.
Sources: https://tibia.fandom.com/wiki/Cooldown; https://wiki.tibiara.com/server/classic_mechanics/; https://www.tibia.com/gameguides/?subtopic=manual (magic section, corroborating)

## [high] Chokepoint tactics rest on an explicit block cap: a defender can block at most 2 incoming (blockable/melee) attacks at a time regardless of shielding skill, and the official manual itself recommends positioning so no more than 2 enemies can attack you simultaneously, ideally with an escape route. Capping simultaneous adjacent attackers via terrain is the intended, developer-endorsed play pattern.
Evidence: Manual verified verbatim in two places: 'no more than 2 attacks can be blocked at a time' and (section 5.3.8, 'Avoid Getting Cornered') 'no matter how high your character's shielding skill, you will not be able to block more than 2 opponents at a time! Always make sure your characters are placed in positions where no more than 2 enemies can attack them at a time'. Caveat: magic damage is never blocked by shielding. Merges claims [1] and [2].
Sources: https://www.tibia.com/gameguides/?subtopic=manual&section=combat

## [high] Body-blocking works because tile occupancy is HARD-blocking in pathfinding, not a soft cost: Map::canWalkTo calls the tile's queryAdd with FLAG_PATHFINDING and returns nullptr on rejection, so an occupied chokepoint tile never enters the A* open list. Push flags then decide who holds a corridor: the dragon is pushable=0 (players cannot shove it), canpushcreatures=1 (it pushes weaker pushable monsters aside — and TFS kills them if the push fails — but never pushes players), and staticattack=90 (stands still ~90% of walk decisions while engaged). Net effect: a knight body-blocking a 1-tile corridor holds a dragon in place; trapping it behind weak monsters fails. A companion claim that occupied tiles are merely penalized at 3x walk cost was REFUTED 0-3 — do not design chokepoints as soft costs.
Evidence: map.cpp verified verbatim: queryAdd(0, creature, 1, FLAG_PATHFINDING) != RETURNVALUE_NOERROR → return nullptr; getPathMatching 'continue's on nullptr so the tile is excluded, while walk costs apply only to passable tiles. Tile::queryAdd returns NOTENOUGHROOM/NOTPOSSIBLE for occupied tiles (player occupants never pushable by monsters). dragon.xml flags verified verbatim; engine cross-checks confirm each flag's semantics including pushCreatures iterating monsters only. Merges claims [14] and [12], plus the refuted soft-cost claim as a design warning.
Sources: https://github.com/otland/forgottenserver/blob/master/src/map.cpp; https://github.com/otland/tfs-old-svn/blob/master/data/monster/Dragons/dragon.xml; https://github.com/otland/tfs-old-svn (game.cpp, monster.cpp, tile.cpp engine semantics)

## [high] Creature AI is structured as multiple independent per-behavior tick accumulators — attackTicks, defenseTicks, yellTicks, targetChangeTicks, each with its own threshold and reset — all fed by a single 1000ms think pulse (EVENT_CREATURE_THINK_INTERVAL). Each behavior has its own cadence/cooldown; nothing shares a clock.
Evidence: monster.h declarations verified verbatim (attackTicks, targetChangeTicks, defenseTicks, yellTicks, targetChangeCooldown); monster.cpp Monster::onThink(interval) delegates to onThinkTarget/onThinkYell/onThinkDefense/doAttacking, each accumulating the interval against its own threshold independently. Design takeaway for game-two: one scheduler pulse, N independent accumulators. Claim [7].
Sources: https://github.com/otland/forgottenserver/blob/master/src/monster.h; https://github.com/otland/forgottenserver/blob/master/src/monster.cpp

## [high] Flee behavior is a pure data-driven threshold with a greedy escape policy: a monster flees exactly when !isSummon() && health <= runAwayHealth (per-creature XML, absolute HP not percent, default 0 = never) && not taunt-locked — no randomness. On flee, pathfinding flips to run-away mode: maxTargetDist = Map::maxViewportX (runs to just past the visible viewport edge, 11 vs client 8), keepDistance=true, fullPathSearch=false — candidate tiles restricted to the half-plane away from the target, so fleeing monsters kite away greedily and can be corner-trapped rather than computing optimal escapes.
Evidence: isFleeing() verified verbatim in monster.h ('return !isSummon() && getHealth() <= mType->info.runAwayHealth && challengeFocusDuration <= 0;'); flee pathfinding block verified verbatim in Monster::getPathSearchParams; monsters.cpp loads runAwayHealth from XML 'runonhealth'; FrozenPathingConditionCall::isInRange with fullPathSearch=false confirms half-plane greedy movement. Merges claims [9] and [5].
Sources: https://github.com/otland/forgottenserver/blob/master/src/monster.h; https://github.com/otland/forgottenserver/blob/master/src/monster.cpp

## [high] The signature 'monster dance' is Monster::getDanceStep: while at attack position with no path step to take, the monster computes Chebyshev distance to target, builds candidate side-steps that preserve that distance (and optionally the attack line), and picks one uniformly at random. Dance frequency is gated per-creature by staticAttackChance (dragon: 90 → dances ~10% of decisions), so shuffle intensity is a data knob per creature type.
Evidence: getDanceStep verified verbatim on master: 'int32_t centerToDist = std::max(distance_x, distance_y);' and 'direction = dirList[uniform_random(0, dirList.size() - 1)];' with tmpDist == centerToDist && canWalkTo filters; call site in getNextStep commented '// target dancing', gated by 'staticAttackChance < uniform_random(1, 100)'. Claim [6] plus the staticattack gloss from [12].
Sources: https://github.com/otland/forgottenserver/blob/master/src/monster.cpp; https://github.com/otland/tfs-old-svn/blob/master/data/monster/Dragons/dragon.xml

## [medium] Ranged creatures attack on a 1-second cadence (an attack-opportunity roll per 1s think tick, chance-gated per spell) and are not movement-locked — they can fire while moving, unlike melee which carries a 2s exhaust.
Evidence: Tibiara verbatim: 'Ranged creatures attack every second and can launch extra attacks while moving.' Corroborated in 7.7-leak-derived code: 1000ms think interval; melee locked via earliestAttackTime = now + 2000; ranged spellBlocks carry no time lock and no movement gate, chance-rolled every tick. Medium: 2-1 vote, secondary source, and the cadence is an opportunity roll rather than a guaranteed shot per second. Claim [19].
Sources: https://wiki.tibiara.com/server/classic_mechanics/; https://github.com/Ezzz-dev/Nostalrius (CipSoft-7.7-derived corroboration)

## [high] Grid-movement feel comes from quantization at two levels. Pathfinding: TFS A* charges diagonal steps MAP_DIAGONALWALKCOST=25 vs cardinal MAP_NORMALWALKCOST=10 — a 2.5x penalty (deliberately above geometric sqrt(2)), so creature paths strongly prefer cardinal moves with rare diagonal cuts. Player speed: base speed is deterministic and linear (s = 109 + level), but EFFECTIVE movement snaps to a small set of discrete per-terrain speed breakpoints (tile-traversal times in 50ms steps), so incremental speed bonuses do nothing until they cross a breakpoint.
Evidence: map.cpp verified verbatim: '|dx| == |dy| → MAP_DIAGONALWALKCOST' in AStarNodes::getMapWalkCost, live in the A* g-cost; map.h constants 10/25. Fandom Speed verified verbatim for both 's = 109 + L' and 'actual movement speed... can only be one of a few discrete values depending on the Speed Breakpoints'; TFS baseSpeed formula independently matches the wiki's pre-9.8 doubled scale; 3+ third-party breakpoint calculators corroborate. Breakpoint thresholds vary per terrain via tile friction. Merges claims [13], [20], [21].
Sources: https://github.com/otland/forgottenserver/blob/master/src/map.cpp; https://tibia.fandom.com/wiki/Speed; https://tibia.fandom.com/wiki/Speed_Breakpoints (corroborating)
