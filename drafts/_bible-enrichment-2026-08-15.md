# Bible de-slop enrichment — proposal (2026-08-15, lyrics-hub harvest)

## 1. Harvested-craft summary

Techniques actually deployed in surviving edits (one line each):

- **Two-text specification (dual register streams):** write two separate text streams with different jobs — ceremonial/incantatory (stream A, phonetic survival, no semantic load) vs plain declarative (stream B, carries every load-bearing word); the switch between them IS the emotional event.
- **Sensory anchors that resist paraphrase:** non-narrative sensory details (a sound, a texture, a temperature) that remain stubbornly physical and cannot be summarized into meaning without loss.
- **Causal withholding:** ban explanatory connectives (because, so, therefore, thus, hence); let the reader connect cause and effect from juxtaposition alone.
- **Found-language specificity:** overheard phrases, unglamorous nouns, trade-specific vocabulary handed verbatim to named characters or institutions as speech; specificity of a mundane technical noun does characterization better than elaborate description.
- **Plain-speech anchor:** key repeated text (mantras, refrains, the lines a reader will say aloud) must use words a tired person could actually say; ornament belongs in surrounding tissue, not in the anchoring lines.
- **Sprung-rhythm stress budget:** fix N stresses per line, each foot beginning on the stress with variable unstressed syllables; delete every word that is neither a stress nor grammatically load-bearing.
- **Institutional speech as charged material:** procedural language from systems of authority (guild charters, temple protocols, guard-rotation orders, tax-ledger entries) is first-class lyric material — write the exact sentence from the form, not a summary of what the form was for.
- **Withholding over asserting (the discrimination boundary):** ambiguity that makes the reader WORK is defensible; intensity that spares the WRITER the work is not — when in doubt, remove rather than add.
- **Cold diction contrast:** insert one technical, procedural, or trade-specific word into an emotional or mythic context; the clinical word creates a temperature drop that makes the surrounding warmth or dread more vivid.

## 2. Audit verdict summary

Two independent audit passes found 15 named tics across the bible. The heaviest:

1. **"which is why" causal-chain tic** — 18+ instances of retroactive cosmic explanation appended to world-facts, often two or three chained per paragraph. The bible EXPLAINS causation where juxtaposition alone would be stronger.
2. **"not X but Y" negation-pivot** — 16+ instances defining things by first saying what they are NOT. A Claude prose fingerprint; most entries are throat-clearing before the real assertion.
3. **Italicized-epigram tic** — nearly every section closes with a freestanding italicized aphorism restating the thesis as a proverb. Creates a predictable bow-on-each-box rhythm.
4. **Every-sentence-load-bearing (no texture/dirt)** — zero throwaway lines, zero sentences existing only for rhythm or color. The AI habit of "maximally efficient prose" reads as airless. Real liturgy accumulates barnacles.
5. **Register flatness across domains** — myth, chronicle, design note, folk-saying, and bureaucratic entry all use the same syntactic texture (compound sentence, em-dash parenthetical, semicoloned elaboration). The Kadravai should not read like the Registry.
6. **Triadic-list as default structure** — ideas packaged in groups of three as the near-universal organizing rhythm, even where two or four would be more natural.
7. **Meta-thesis intrusion** — the narrator periodically breaks from the world's voice to tell the reader what the passage MEANS ("this is the X of the Y", "the X is the point").
8. **Slotted-template uniformity** — every god entry, culture entry, and region entry follows an identical internal structure with the same sentence-level texture in each slot.
9. **Rigid template cadence** (auditor 2) — "Virtue: X. Vice: Y. Player-flavor: Z." Every culture entry in lockstep; the reader predicts the next entry's structure blind.
10. **No sensory dirt** — the creation myth has zero smell, zero stumble, zero inexplicable inherited detail. Every image is thematically pointed. Real liturgy accumulates useless prohibitions nobody remembers the reason for.
11. **Over-neat triads** (auditor 2) — three Laws, three pantheon rules, three counts of necromancy, three super-temples, three forbidden tracks. Real theology argues about how many things there are.
12. **List-shaped prose** — faction descriptions as bullet lists with identical sentence structure, where narrative should live.

## 3. THE EDITS

### Edit 0 — Section 3.1 The Loom Liturgy

**Technique:** Two-text specification (dual register streams)

**Rationale:** The liturgy is described as CHANTED but reads in the same informed-prose voice as everything else. This edit splits it into two registers: a ceremonial stream (the opening declarations, built for phonetic survival) and a plain narrative stream (the origin of Nakhoro). The chanter's stage direction creates an audible gap between the two — the transition IS the emotional event. Also injects "dirt" (a performance instruction no one remembers the theological reason for) that the audit flagged as absent.

```
BEFORE
Before the first thing, there was the Unsaid — **Vesse**, the shoreless water that is not water, in which everything that could ever be lay dissolved with everything that could never be. It was not empty. It was full past bearing, and silent — until some part of it *heard itself*. The part that heard drew apart from the part that was heard, and in the space between them a word became possible. The first name spoken was the speaker's own, and by saying it, he was: **Nakhoro**, the First Speaker, the one who said himself.
```

```
AFTER
Before the first thing: the Unsaid. **Vesse.** The shoreless water that is not water, in which everything that could ever be lay dissolved with everything that could never be. Full past bearing. Silent.

[*Here the chanter waits until no one is breathing.*]

Some part of it heard itself. The part that heard drew apart from the part that was heard, and in the space between them a word became possible. The first name spoken was the speaker's own — and by saying it, he was: **Nakhoro**, the First Speaker, the one who said himself.
```

### Edit 1 — Section 3.1 The Loom Liturgy

**Technique:** Sensory anchors that resist paraphrase

**Rationale:** The original runs god-introductions as pure thesis-work (each sentence introduces a god AND explains their cosmic role AND delivers a poetic image). This injects inherited liturgical dirt — a bracketed chanter's note about river-order vs delta-order that serves no theological purpose but signals a living, argued tradition. The detail resists paraphrase: you cannot summarize "the argument has outlived three deans" into a theme.

```
BEFORE
Then Nakhoro spoke the Long Words, and the Long Words are the gods. A word of fire, burning and glad: **Khelsurat**, the First Flame, the sun that was a person.

```
AFTER
Then Nakhoro spoke the Long Words, and the Long Words are the gods. [*The chanter here names them in the river-order, east bank to west; delta congregations reverse this, and the argument has outlived three deans.*] A word of fire, burning and glad: **Khelsurat**, the First Flame, the sun that was a person.

### Edit 4 — Section 3.2 The Severing of the Door

**Technique:** Causal withholding

**Rationale:** The original uses "this is why" — the single most frequent tic in the document (18+ instances). The causal connective does the reader's inferential work for them. The edit states both facts as flat declarations; the reader connects them without being told to. Shorter, harder, trusts the audience.

```
BEFORE
this is why the dead of Suvareth have legal standing, and why every funerary rite is drafted like a writ.
```

```
AFTER
The dead of Suvareth have legal standing. Every funerary rite is drafted like a writ.
```

### Edit 6 — Section 2. The Naming Language

**Technique:** Found-language specificity

**Rationale:** The original is an italicized epigram in the narrator's voice — the "bow on each box" tic the audit flagged. The edit replaces it with a found-object: a specific rhyme from a specific trade (Reed pilots), chalked on a specific thing (a prayer-hull), at a specific place (Ossel). The world speaks in its own voice rather than the narrator delivering a tidy summary. The specificity does characterization the epigram cannot.

```
BEFORE
*A name mispronounced is a door knocked on at the wrong house.*
```

```
AFTER
A Reed pilot's rhyme, chalked on a prayer-hull at Ossel: *say Sildra with Kadrash's tongue and see who comes to the door.*
```

### Edit 7 — Section 5.3 The Half-Passage

**Technique:** Plain-speech anchor

**Rationale:** The original stacks metaphors ("corridor instead of a door", "dying is not an event but a journey", "turned around") and uses the "not X but Y" pivot the audit flagged 16+ times. The edit keeps "corridor" (it earns its place) but strips the rest to plain-speech a tired person could say aloud: "A corridor has two directions." The landing phrase ("pulled back by its own name") uses words of one syllable except "its own name" — the anchoring line is plain enough to live in someone's mouth.

```
BEFORE
Because Kadrash's stroke made death a *corridor* instead of a door, dying is not an event but a **journey** — and a journey can be turned around. A soul whose strands are recovered and re-knotted before it completes the twelve chambers has not "come back from death"; it simply *never finished dying*.
```

```
AFTER
Kadrash's stroke made death a corridor. A corridor has two directions. A soul whose strands are recovered and re-knotted before it completes the twelve chambers has simply *never finished dying* — turned around mid-descent, pulled back by its own name.
```

### Edit 9 — Section 7.1 Humankind (Kadravai)

**Technique:** Sprung-rhythm stress budget

**Rationale:** The audit flagged register flatness: the Kadravai (drum-folk, oral culture) should not read in the same compound-sentence narrator voice as the Sildaruni (scribal bureaucracy). This edit breaks the long flowing sentences into short percussive declarations — "Herders. Caravan-masters." — mimicking drum-beat rhythm. The "Virtue/Vice" template slot (flagged as rigid-template uniformity) is replaced with a culture-specific formulation: "Their word for cowardice is *sitting down mid-song*." The culture's own register infects the entry.

```
BEFORE
**The Kadravai** — drum-confederacies of the southern desert (§9.2). Nomad herders and caravan-masters who hold that the world is a rhythm and settlements are missed beats. They keep the Drum Account, bury their dead standing (a standing soul walks the corridor faster), and are the only culture that *likes* Kadrash — the desert teaches his arithmetic early. Every Kadravai learns the **night-count**, the Long Dark counting-song; the third-syllable naming rite is a solo vigil against the dark. Virtue: endurance. Vice: contempt for walls that extends to the people behind them. Player-flavor: rangers, drum-priests, caravan guards, the world's best Fray-watchers.
```

```
AFTER
**The Kadravai** — drum-confederacies of the southern desert (§9.2). Herders. Caravan-masters. They hold the world is a rhythm and settlements are missed beats. They keep the Drum Account. They bury their dead standing — a standing soul walks the corridor faster. The only culture that *likes* Kadrash; the desert teaches his arithmetic early. Every Kadravai learns the **night-count**, the Long Dark counting-song; the third-syllable naming rite is a solo vigil against the dark. Their word for cowardice is *sitting down mid-song*. Player-flavor: rangers, drum-priests, caravan guards, the world's best Fray-watchers.
```

### Edit 10 — Section 7.1 Humankind (Sildaruni)

**Technique:** Institutional speech as charged material

**Rationale:** The original uses the narrator's voice to describe the culture's vice ("certification-worship") with a parenthetical explanation. The edit lets the institution speak for itself: "They say *a thing not written has not quite happened*" — the exact sentence a Sildaruni would use, handed verbatim as institutional speech. The fear is the material, not the narrator's label for it. Also removes the "Virtue/Vice" template labels the audit flagged as rigid-template cadence.

```
BEFORE
Virtue: continuity. Vice: certification-worship ("a thing not written has not quite happened"). Post-Heresy, a people with a scar where their pride was: it was *their* king who tried to unsay heaven. Player-flavor: the default civilization — priests, scribes, river-army soldiers, tomb-pilots.
```

```
AFTER
They say *a thing not written has not quite happened*, and build their lives around the fear of it. Post-Heresy, a people with a scar where their pride was: it was *their* king who tried to unsay heaven. Player-flavor: the default civilization — priests, scribes, river-army soldiers, tomb-pilots.
```

### Edit 12 — Section 4.3 The Dead and the Compound

**Technique:** Withholding over asserting (the discrimination boundary)

**Rationale:** The original's final sentence ("It is standing proof that the pantheon is a *grammar*, still generating new sentences") is a meta-thesis intrusion — the narrator telling the reader what the passage MEANS. The audit flagged this pattern explicitly. The edit cuts it. The compound's significance (proof that the pantheon is generative) is already implicit in the preceding material; stating it spares the writer the work of trusting the reader. The passage is stronger ending on "a knot both cults resent" — an image with tension, not an explanation.

```
BEFORE
The compound is the closest Hezreth has ever come to existing in a mouth — the veiled god's single vanity, and the reason he permits a knot both cults resent. It is standing proof that the pantheon is a *grammar*, still generating new sentences.
```

```
AFTER
The compound is the closest Hezreth has ever come to existing in a mouth — the veiled god's single vanity, and the reason he permits a knot both cults resent.
```

### Edit 13 — Section 6.6 The Heresy Age

**Technique:** Cold diction contrast

**Rationale:** The audit flagged register flatness: the chronicle should sound like a historian, not a poet. This edit inserts a scholarly dating dispute in dry procedural language ("the Registry counts from... the Vaultwardens from... Nine months separate the two reckonings. Neither temple has yielded.") The clinical tone creates a temperature drop before the mythic weight of the Lantern King's introduction. The trade vocabulary (Registry, Sequestration of the Rolls, reckonings) is charged material from the fiction's own bureaucracy — institutional speech doing characterization work.

```
BEFORE
The wound the present still limps on. **Suvakhelan IV**, called afterward **the Lantern King**, inherited the Sildarun high throne young, brilliant, and — the surviving fragments genuinely suggest — sincere.
```

```
AFTER
The wound the present still limps on. [*Scholars disagree on dating: the Registry counts from the Sequestration of the Rolls; the Vaultwardens from the first unbound burial. Nine months separate the two reckonings. Neither temple has yielded.*] **Suvakhelan IV**, called afterward **the Lantern King**, inherited the Sildarun high throne young, brilliant, and — the surviving fragments genuinely suggest — sincere.
```

### Edit 14 — Section 6.3 The Long Dark

**Technique:** Sensory anchors that resist paraphrase

**Rationale:** The audit flagged "no sensory dirt" — the creation/catastrophe material has zero smell, zero stumble, zero irreducible physical detail. This appends one: "The glass at the Anvil's edge still rings when struck — a clear tone, middle C or near it, three thousand years cold." The detail is a sensory anchor (a sound, a pitch, a duration) that cannot be paraphrased into a theme. It resists summary. It is also inherited-useless-detail: nobody knows WHY the glass rings at middle C, and the passage does not explain. The "or near it" is deliberate imprecision — a scholar's hedge, not a narrator's omniscience.

```
BEFORE
**Stratum:** the *scars* — glass plains, hills folded like cloth, crater-lakes where a god stood in wrath; and the *cinderfalls*, where pieces of the burning First Flame struck. Scar-sites do not heal and cannot be built on; the Weave there is fused, not woven.
```

```
AFTER
**Stratum:** the *scars* — glass plains, hills folded like cloth, crater-lakes where a god stood in wrath; and the *cinderfalls*, where pieces of the burning First Flame struck. Scar-sites do not heal and cannot be built on; the Weave there is fused, not woven. The glass at the Anvil's edge still rings when struck — a clear tone, middle C or near it, three thousand years cold.
```
