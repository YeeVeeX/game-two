# SUVARETH — The Raft of Names
## World Bible, First Canon Edition

*A founding document for a long-lived 2D world. Everything in this book is true in-world unless marked as disputed. Everything disputed is disputed on purpose.*

---

## 1. Overview & Design Pillars

Suvareth is a woven world: a raft of named things floating on a shoreless sea of everything that was never named. It is not a world that *is* — it is a world that is *kept*. Every sunrise is a victory someone won overnight. Every law, levee, and living body is stitchwork, and stitches fray.

On the surface, Suvareth wears the familiar skin of European fantasy: dragons in the ash-hills, brute warbands on the trade roads, giants asleep under the highlands, the walking dead in the fens, fae things in the reeds that want your name. Underneath runs a different engine — upkeep, speech, and debt. The crossover is the point: the bestiary is European; the physics is liturgical.

Three laws make this world distinct. They are design pillars first and theology second; nothing may be added to this canon that violates them.

**Law I — Order is rent, not right.** The world's coherence (*sereth*, the Weave) is not a default state. It is maintained daily against *vesk*, the Fray — the patient unraveling that waits in the outer sea, in deep places, and in every untended thing. The sun is relit each dawn because someone carried it through the dark. Temples are pumping stations of order; if the rites stop, the district frays — crops fail, names slip, the dead stand up wrong. Gameplay consequence: maintenance made visible — vigils, offerings, festival deadlines, decaying wards — and villains who need not invade, only interrupt.

**Law II — Nothing exists that was not named.** Speech is the world's operating system. The world was spoken into being, and every created thing still carries its true name as a handle. Magic is *quotation* — borrowing the grammar of creation for a sentence or two. Writing is stored speech; erasure is violence; a secret name is a master key. Gameplay consequence: spell words, runes, name-passwords at gates, inscription and erasure as core verbs, and an economy of scribes, notaries, and forgers.

**Law III — Every monster has an ancestor in heaven.** Every creature family descends from a specific god, age, or catastrophe, and a learned character can read a monster the way a geologist reads rock strata. Dragons are cinders of a dead sun; the brute-folk are discharged soldiers of a war their god no longer fights; the fae are sentences the Namer never finished. Gameplay consequence: the bestiary is a history book — where a creature spawns tells you what happened there, and lore knowledge is player power.

Over all three laws lies the Withdrawal: the gods of Suvareth are real, active, and *absent*. They do not walk on screen. They answer through temples, dreams, omens, weather, and the slow bookkeeping of fate — never in person, for reasons the myths explain and the canon rules protect. Their fingerprints are on every tile of the world; their faces are on none of it.

Myth sections below are written as myth — flavor text, epitaphs, and quest dialogue will be quarried from them. Systems sections are design prose. The final two sections translate the cosmology into the mechanical conventions of a grid-based, hub-and-spoke, skill-through-use RPG, then lock the canon so years of future content can grow without contradiction.

*The world holds because someone holds it.*

## 2. The Naming Language

Every proper noun in this canon derives from one constructed tongue, **Vessic** — "the speech left over from the Saying." In-fiction, Vessic is the residue of the language the Namer used to speak the world. Out of fiction, it is the naming toolkit every future contributor must use. Names not derivable from this section are not canon.

### 2.1 Phonology

**Consonants:** t, k, d, g, b, m, n, s, z, sh, v, h, kh (as in *loch*), th (as in *thin*), r, l, w, y, and the glottal stop, written **'** — which appears only in the divine register, joining compound names. **Vowels:** a, e, i, o, u; doubled vowels occur only in liturgical chant. **Syllable shape:** (C)(C)V(C). Permitted onset clusters: **dr, vr, khr, thr, sk, sv**. Forbidden: three-consonant clusters; **thr** followed by **k** in the same word (a chant-taboo said to "cut the tongue"). Words never end in **h** or a cluster. **Stress** falls on the first syllable of the root, not the affix: *SIL-dra*, *UL-veth*. **Register rule:** the glottal compound (X'Y) is reserved for gods and god-touched artifacts; a mortal taking one commits blasphemy — used exactly once in recorded history (§6).

### 2.4 Name tables by culture

**Sildarun (river kingdom) — theophoric names.** Pattern: [god-root] + [verb/state], "the god does X for me." Male ends in -an, -at, -et; female in -ess, -ura, -is. Examples: *Hezuvan* ("the Veiled One shelters"), *Savmurat* ("light turns the soil"), *Dekhaness* ("measured and found whole"), *Silkhura* ("the river remembers"), *Nakhotet* ("small name, may it grow"). Kings take a fifth name at coronation, always containing **suv** (the crown's duty: hold).

**Kadravai (desert drum-folk) — struck names.** Two hard syllables echoing a drum figure; doubled consonants prized: *Avekka, Ruzda, Kammat, Drovekk, Zanna*. A third syllable is earned only by standing a night watch alone.

**Markfolk (the Hovanmark) — worn names.** Vessic roots ground down by a colder mouth (kh→h, th→t): *Halden* (from *khel-dan*, hearth-holder), *Bralda, Hodrim, Tessa, Uvebrand*. Surnames are trades or trees: *Nine-Pines, Ropewalker, Barrowward*.

**Free Reeds (delta league) — water names.** Liquid, vowel-forward; two syllables in trade, three at home: *Ossel, Virel, Ionna, Auvess, Melor*. A Reed's full name includes their boat's name; selling the boat changes the name.

**The Molded (freed constructs) — petition names.** A single root plus **-et**, granted by the Registry one clause at a time as personhood is earned: *Suvet, Nakhet, Ovet*. A fully-named Molded carries two roots; none has yet earned three.

**Divine register.** Gods' common names are single compounds (Ulveth, Savra, Kadrash). Their *deep names* — the true handles — were withdrawn from the world at the Hush and are written nowhere. This is a canon lock, not an omission: no future text may reveal a god's deep name.

*A name mispronounced is a door knocked on at the wrong house.*

## 3. The Creation Cycle

*What follows is the Loom Liturgy, the most widely chanted of the three canonical creation accounts, told as the temples tell it. Two rival accounts are summarized after; by ancient rule no council may declare between them.*

### 3.1 The Loom Liturgy

Before the first thing, there was the Unsaid — **Vesse**, the shoreless water that is not water, in which everything that could ever be lay dissolved with everything that could never be. It was not empty. It was full past bearing, and silent — until some part of it *heard itself*. The part that heard drew apart from the part that was heard, and in the space between them a word became possible. The first name spoken was the speaker's own, and by saying it, he was: **Nakhoro**, the First Speaker, the one who said himself.

And Nakhoro was one, and found that one is a wound. So he did the only thing a speaker can do against silence — he kept talking. He spoke himself into many: *the One Who Said Himself into Multitudes*. Everything that has ever existed is a clause of that first long sentence, still being uttered.

But words spoken over the Unsaid sink. So before all else he said **suv** — *hold* — and the word did not sink. It spread upon the deep like a floor of reeds, and it held. That word, still holding, is the world: **Suvareth**, the Raft of Names. Everything that stands, stands on a single ancient syllable of stubbornness. This is the First Mercy, and it is why the holiest act in Suvareth is neither praise nor sacrifice but *maintenance*.

Then Nakhoro spoke the Long Words, and the Long Words are the gods. A word of fire, burning and glad: **Khelsurat**, the First Flame, the sun that was a person. A word of water that gathers: **Sildra**. A word of shaping: **Tekhur**. A word of binding: **Zolvah**, who ties. A word of measure: **Dekharu**, for a drifting word becomes a lie. A word of severing: **Kadrash**, for a weaver needs a knife. A word of carrying: **Savra**, who bears what cannot bear itself. A word of turning-under: **Mureth**, who takes the spent and gives back soil. And a word of opening: **Ovet**, the Door, through whom the raw Unsaid could flow inward and be woven into new things — for a world that admits nothing new is only a well-made tomb.

Two more beings were never spoken on purpose. Between every word there was a pause, and one day the accumulated silence stood up and had a shape: **Hezreth**, the Veiled One, the unsaid *inside* the said, whom no image fits because he was never uttered. And every word came back off the far dark slightly wrong, and the crooked returns gathered themselves and giggled: **Havir**, the Echo, whom the Speaker could not unmake, because you cannot unsay a thing you never said.

The gods took up the threads and wove. Their weaving is **sereth**, the Weave: the lawful pattern by which water runs downhill, wounds close, and each morning follows its night. Where the Weave's edge met the Unsaid, the threads worked loose and whispered apart: **vesk**, the Fray. The liturgy insists: the Fray is not a rival god, not a will — it is what happens where the said meets the unsaid, the price of having edges.

Each god made servants. Tekhur shaped the **Gorai**, the giants, who raised the highlands as formwork for the sky. Kadrash mustered the **Vakhur** from clay and shout, soldiers before there was a war. Nakhoro discarded ten thousand unfinished phrasings; some crawled off the page before he could unsay them — the fae, **the Unfinished**. But none of these could *say new things*. Last of all the Speaker made the short words: humankind — small, so the Raft could bear many; brief, so no error would last; and to them alone he gave the trick he trusted no god with twice. A human being is the only made thing that can make with its mouth.

### 3.2 The Severing of the Door

Through Ovet's open door the Unsaid flowed in and was woven: new souls, new springs, new rain. But through the same door the Fray *listened* — and began to learn words, and to unsay them one at a time. A shepherd's dog was forgotten out of existence between evening and morning, and only the rope it had worn remembered. Then a well. Then a village's name, and with the name, the village. The gods met in the Quarrel of the Door, nine years of shouting.

Ovet would not close. This must be told fairly, the liturgy says, *for the Door was not proud and not foolish*: a closed world receives no new souls, no renewal — only a slow going-stale. Ovet stood in the doorway and said: *while I am open, we bleed; if I close, we only die longer.* And the gods could not choose, for both sides were right, and gods do not vote.

Kadrash chose. Not from envy — the liturgies are unanimous and emphatic, for this is the hinge of all theology — but from *arithmetic*. He loved the world with a soldier's love, which counts. He counted the villages unsaid that month, and the months remaining, and he went to the Door by night with the blade Nakhoro had made him, and he cut Ovet into twelve pieces, and the Door closed with the falling of the body.

The world was saved and broken in one stroke. The Fray's whisper stopped at the shut door. But the dead — who had always slipped back through Ovet into the Unsaid, to dissolve and return as rain returns — now had nowhere to go. They thickened in the air like smoke in a shuttered room. The first ghosts date from the morning after the murder: *that was the day death stopped working.*

Sildra went out to gather him: twelve pieces, scattered the width of the world. She carried them one by one toward the low place in the west, weeping as she went, and the furrow her dragging grief cut in the world filled with her tears and flows still — the great river **Sil** is the drag-mark of a god's funeral, and its yearly flood is Sildra walking her grief-road again. As she carried the pieces she sang to them, and her laments were the first spells any mortal ever overheard. All mortal magic descends from a funeral song.

But what is severed cannot be made one; even gods obey grammar. So Zolvah knotted — not the pieces into a body, but into a *house*: twelve pieces buried each deeper than the last, knotted on one cord, down to the deepest chamber, where they laid the heart. The corpse became architecture — where a god's hand lay, a hall; where his spine, a stair — the **Undervault**, the world beneath the world, a door remade as a dwelling. A *slow* door.

At the setting of the twelfth piece, the buried god woke. Not as Ovet. What woke was patient, dark-eyed, vast in a rooted way, and it knew everything the dead know: *I am the Door still. But now the way through me is long, and none shall pass but at a walking pace, and all shall be weighed at my heart.* Thus **Ulveth**, the Kept-Below: the murdered god who is the underworld, whose body you will walk through when you die.

His first act was a trial: he judged the murder of himself — the only case in which victim and judge were one, which is why his rulings bind absolutely. The verdict is recited at every funeral: *the cut was needful, and the cut is unforgivable, and both stand.* The sentence: the Debt. Every mortal death is accounted a wound of Kadrash's blade — his stroke made death a locked corridor instead of an open door — and every soul's passage is paid from the war-god's inexhaustible, unpayable account. Kadrash may not enter the Undervault until the Debt clears. It will never clear. The dead arrive below not as beggars but as *creditors*: this is why the dead of Suvareth have legal standing, and why every funerary rite is drafted like a writ.

Renewal resumed — routed now through death. The Unsaid's raw newness enters the world only up through the Undervault, at the pace of a funeral procession: spring rises out of graves, seed out of turned soil, souls out of the deep door. This is why Mureth's compost is holy, why farmers plant over their ancestors, and why a world without death would starve in a generation.

### 3.3 The Long Dark and the Lamp

The Fray did not forgive the shut door. It had learned to speak, and now it spoke *back*: out of the rim came the vesk-tide, an unmaking host, a war of erasure against everything named. This was the God-War, which mortals call the Long Dark; the liturgy keeps its two great griefs. First: the gods fought at full weight upon the Weave, and the Weave could not bear them — every place a god stood in wrath is a scar to this day. Second: **Khelsurat died.** The First Flame went to the rim and burned against the tide, day upon day, spending himself so the host could not cross, until nothing was left of him to burn. His cooled body hangs in the night sky still — the moon is the corpse of the first sun, which is why moonlight warms nothing. His falling cinders, scattered burning across the world, remembered being part of a god of fire and pride: they are the dragons, every one an ember of a dead sun, which is why dragons hoard warmth and gold and cannot abide being unremembered.

The tide was turned, and the world was lightless. So Tekhur took from the cold corpse the last live cinder, the heart-coal, and forged around it a lamp: **the Ember**, the sun that burns above Suvareth now. Mark this, for it divides Suvareth's heaven from every other: *the sun in the sky is not a god.* It is a made thing, and it gutters; it cannot rekindle itself. Each dusk **Savra** — the carrier, who volunteered before the forge was cool — takes it aboard her woven vessel, the Little Raft, and bears it down the western descent, through the twelve chambers of the buried god, through the outer dark where the tide's last remnant, the serpent **Ithves**, strikes at the light hour upon hour; and at midnight, in the deepest chamber, she lays the guttering Ember against Ulveth's heart — the one fire deep enough — and the lamp rekindles, and the buried god is warmed. The mystery-formula of that meeting is the most guarded sentence in the temples: *the light rests in the deep; the deep rests in the light.* Then Savra fights her way back up the eastern stair, and if she wins, there is a dawn. The liturgy forbids the word "always" here, and says instead: *there has been a dawn every morning so far.*

### 3.4 The Hush

After the war the gods found the Weave too torn to walk on: their full presence — the weight of a Long Word standing in the world — now split hills and boiled lakes. Worse, the Fray had learned names during the war, even divine ones, and had come near to unsaying a god outright. So Hezreth, the never-uttered, taught the others his own nature: to stand behind the Weave rather than upon it, present in pattern, absent in person. One by one the Long Words withdrew — this is the Hush — and covenanted to act only through threads: temple and omen, dream and weather, priest and rite. They are not gone. They are *load-bearing*, woven into the underside of everything, which is precisely why they cannot come on stage: the puppeteer cannot step into the puppet show without ripping the cloth.

Nakhoro went further: he gathered the deep names of all the gods — the true handles the Fray had begun to learn — and carried them beyond the rim of the world, where no listening reaches, and has not spoken since. His last words are not recorded; the temples hold, on pain of heresy, that they are not *forgotten* but *kept*. Mortals inherited the watch. The commission read at every coronation descends from the gods' parting word, and it fits in eight words: *The gods keep the world. You keep the keeping.*

### 3.5 The two rival accounts

The **Drum Account** of the Kadravai teaches that the world was never spoken but *struck*: existence is a rhythm held against silence, Nakhoro the first beat, every living heart a drummer keeping the world's time. The **Flood Account** of the Free Reeds teaches that nothing was made at all: the Unsaid is a flood slowly settling, the world a sandbar of temporary certainty, the gods merely the largest things stranded on it. All three accounts run the same rites and fear the same Fray. The Concord of the Three Tellings rules that all three are true, that their contradiction is a property of mortal grammar and not of the world, and that any priest who reopens the question owes the other two temples a year of labor.

> **Design note — what this transmutes.** The Unsaid is the primordial-ocean archetype (menace *and* sole source of renewal). Creation-by-speech makes the name-magic system cosmological bedrock. The Severing transmutes the founding divine murder and dismemberment while diverging deliberately from the familiar beats: the motive is triage, not jealousy; the victim is a door, not a harmony-figure; the pieces become underworld *architecture*, not world-elements; and the result is death-as-debt and a creditor-afterlife, the killer an eternal debtor rather than an exiled villain. The nightly Ember-run transmutes the solar-barque cycle, sharpened by one original move: the sun is a *lamp*, because the real sun-god is already dead. The Hush transmutes divine withdrawal and gives it a mechanical reason (divine weight tears the Weave). The rival accounts transmute the coexistence of multiple true cosmogonies. Every choice serves Law I, II, or III.

## 4. The Pantheon

Suvareth's gods are the Long Words: utterances of the First Speaker that have never stopped being said. Three rules govern them everywhere in canon.

**They are absent by covenant.** Since the Hush, no god manifests bodily; they act through weather, dream, omen, rite, and the behavior of their creature-descendants. Content that puts a god on screen violates canon (§14).

**They merge without dissolving.** Two gods may be invoked as a compound — a third being existing *alongside* its constituents, not instead of them, with its own temper and rites, dissolvable by formal liturgy. Theologians call this **knotting**. Cults compete to promote or dissolve knots; a successful knot rearranges real cult-economy power. A live mechanic, not backstory.

**They are local in strength.** A god is strongest where its threads are thickest — Sildra along her river, Ulveth underground everywhere. Distance from one's god is a measurable spiritual condition; travelers buy portable shrines for a reason.

### 4.1 The Greater Words

**NAKHORO, the First Speaker** — *the absent god of naming, records, and the unsaid word.*
Domain: language, true names, scripture, silence. Personality: unknowable by design; remembered as endlessly, wearily generous — a god who talked because stopping meant being alone. Relationships: parent-of-all; his silence since the Hush is the pantheon's central wound. Cult: the **Registry**, the scribe-priesthood that keeps the Rolls of Names and licenses all name-magic. No statues: his shrines hold an empty lectern and an open blank book. Followers: archivists, notaries, name-hunters in grey, ink-stained vestments. Creatures: none living — but every written ward, rune, and golem-word traces to him; the **Molded** (§7) are animated by Registry-script.

**ULVETH, the Kept-Below** — *the murdered Door; lord of the Undervault, judge of the dead.*
Domain: death-as-passage, judgment, buried wealth, patience. Personality: unhurried, exact, courteous the way courts are courteous; incapable of forgetting; not cruel — a being who *is* a building has no use for cruelty. Relationships: was Ovet; holds the Debt over Kadrash; warms the Ember nightly with Savra — the tenderest bond in the pantheon, the carrier and the corpse, each the other's only regular company. Cult: the **Vaultwardens**, embalmer-priests and funeral notaries; their temples are half bank, half courthouse, and always have a staircase going down that visitors may not use. Followers: black-and-ochre vestments, keys at the throat, faces painted with a single vertical seam. Creatures: the lawful dead — tomb-wights on honest guard, funerary jackal-hounds, the Undervault's gate-keepers. (Lawless undead are *not* his; see Mureth and §8 — a distinction that drives whole questlines.)

**KADRASH, the Severing Hand** — *war, surgery, harvest-cutting, necessary loss; the god in debt.*
Domain: every cut that must be made — battle, amputation, pruning, execution. Personality: grim arithmetic and absolute follow-through; the god who loves the world enough to hurt it and refuses to pretend otherwise; despises glory, honors veterans. Relationships: killer of Ovet; barred from the Undervault until the Debt clears; audited by Dekharu; Sildra has not spoken to him since the funeral, *and their silence is a formal liturgical object* — rites needing both are routed through Zolvah. Cult: the **Order of the Paid Edge**, martial priests who bless no war until its cost is read aloud; every temple keeps a ledger of everyone its congregation has ever killed. Followers: rust-red sashes, blunted ceremonial knives; they may not own a weapon they have not named. Creatures: the **Vakhur** war-brutes (his discharged legions, §7–8) and the **kadrim**, red-eyed carrion birds said to be receipts.

**SILDRA, the Gatherer** — *the river, mourning, mercy, assembly of what is scattered.*
Domain: flowing water, grief, healing, the finding of lost things. Personality: grief as competence; the god you call when something must be *found and carried home*. Relationships: chief mourner of Ovet; estranged from Kadrash; mother of the river-spirits; namesake of the kingdom of Sildarun. Cult: the **Silt Mothers**, midwife-embalmers presiding over both ends of life; their riverside temples run the ferry-guilds and the flood-almanac. Followers: blue-green shawls, hair worn wet in ceremony, lament-singing as literal spellcraft. Creatures: river-spirits, healing leech-swarms, the sacred silt-eels that carry funeral barges; the drowned unmourned rise as *her grievance*, not her servants.

**TEKHUR, the Kilnfather** — *shaping, forges, masonry, the made world.*
Domain: craft, construction, fire-as-tool, the dignity of made things. Personality: taciturn, literal, incapable of metaphor (liturgical jokes abound); measures worth in joints and tolerances; the only god the Molded pray to by choice. Relationships: forged the Ember around Khelsurat's heart-coal — his masterwork and his grief; made the Gorai and counts their diminishment his failure; quarrels eternally with Havir, who un-trues his lines. Cult: the **Kilnwrights**, guild-temples where apprenticeship is liturgy and ordination is making one flawless thing. Followers: leather aprons over kiln-orange robes, guild-brands on the forearm. Creatures: the Gorai, forge-salamanders, gargoyle-wards on old temples, and — by later, contested doctrine — the Molded.

**DEKHARU, the Measurer** — *weights, law, thresholds, the scale at the heart of the world.*
Domain: judgment, contracts, standards, fate-as-audit. Personality: serene, incorruptible, faintly terrifying; the only god Ithves fears, because the serpent cannot make Dekharu miscount. Relationships: assessor in Ulveth's court at the Weighing (§5); audits the Debt; certifies kings. Cult: the **House of the Level**, magistrate-priests who calibrate every scale, coin-die, and survey-chain in the kingdoms; excommunication means no contract you sign binds — civil death. Followers: white-and-lead vestments, brass weights carried as rosaries. Creatures: the **dekhim**, sphinx-like threshold beasts that pose no riddles but simply *know whether you are lying*, and the oath-wasps that nest in courthouse eaves.

**SAVRA, the Lightbearer** — *the carried sun, endurance, vigils, hope-as-discipline.*
Domain: dawn, night-watches, couriers, everyone who carries what cannot carry itself. Personality: tired and utterly reliable; the liturgies never call her radiant — they call her *punctual*. Relationships: nightly companion of Ulveth; bearer of Tekhur's lamp; eternal enemy of Ithves, whom she has fought every night since the Long Dark and never once hated ("the serpent is only the dark doing its job"). Cult: the **Vigilant**, whose rite is the through-night vigil; every walled town's night watch is nominally her congregation. Followers: grey travel-cloaks over gold, a shuttered lantern at the hip that is never allowed to go out. Creatures: dawn-larks, lamp-moths, and the **savrim** — small psychopomp lights that guide echoes (§5) home to their corpses at night.

**HEZRETH, the Veiled One** — *the hidden, the pause, the unsaid-within-the-said.*
Domain: mystery, privacy, secrets; secondarily thieves and confessors. Personality: composed of listening; never depicted, never described; every silence in a conversation is his attendance. Relationships: never spoken, so never named to the Fray — the only god it cannot begin to unsay, which made him architect of the Hush; all other gods now stand inside his art. Cult: the **Order of the Drawn Curtain**, confessor-priests bound to die rather than repeat what they hear; their shrines are empty rooms with two chairs. Followers: undyed veils; they answer questions with questions as devotional practice. Creatures: none of his own — instead, anything *may* be his, which is the point. Folk doctrine says one animal in every herd is Hezreth's ear.

### 4.2 The Lesser Words

**ZOLVAH, the Knot-Mother** — *binding, birth, oaths, marriage, the cord.*
She tied the Undervault together; she ties souls to bodies at birth (the **zol-knot**, load-bearing for §5), oaths to speakers, gods to gods (all divine knotting is performed "in her hands"). Warm, implacable; keeps every promise made *to* her and collects on every promise made *through* her. Cult: midwives, rope-makers, marriage-brokers, oath-registrars. Creatures: knot-serpents (harmless, sacred, uncuttable) and the **cord-hags** — horrors that unravel oaths for a price (her excommunicates, not her servants).

**MURETH, the Gleaner** — *decay, compost, harvest's end, the honest rot.*
Decomposition as sacrament: the turning-under by which the spent becomes the fertile. Humble, cheerful, universally underestimated — and the liturgy's dark hinge: *all necromancy is stolen from Mureth*. Undeath is his sacrament interrupted, rot arrested halfway and put to work; nothing angers this mild god but that. Cult: the **Gleaners**, farmer-priests of the charnel-gardens and bone-orchards (bones to Ulveth, flesh to the fields — the Twofold Burial). Followers: brown robes, seed-pouches, sickles worn blunt-side out. Creatures: carrion-beetle swarms, bog-moths, barrow-moles — and, as his *stolen* children, every ghoul and rot-zombie the necromantic counterculture raises (§10).

**HAVIR, the Echo** — *mirrors, jokes, doubles, the crooked return.*
Reflection, mimicry, satire, gambling — everything that comes back changed. The unmade god: never spoken, only bounced; outside the covenant (he never swore the Hush; he finds absence funnier); the pantheon's licensed irritant and, the mystics whisper, its emergency spare — the Fray cannot target him because he has no original to unsay. Cult: none formally; every actor, gambler, and satirist tithes him a first coin or a first laugh. Creatures: doppel-wisps, mockingbeasts, mirror-mites — and the **hollow twins**, born when Havir echoes a person before Zolvah has finished knotting them.

**ITHVES, the Last Unsaying** — *not worshipped; catalogued.*
The remnant of the vesk-tide: the serpent of the outer dark that assaults the Ember every night on Savra's descent. Not a god — a Long Silence, the Fray's most finished sentence. It cannot be killed, because it is not alive; it can only be *held*, nightly, forever. The rim-cults that feed it names commit the setting's deepest crime (§10). Creatures: everything vesk-touched — the Frayed (§8), null-hounds, the unsaid-wraiths of the world's rim.

### 4.3 The Dead and the Compound

**KHELSURAT, the First Flame** *(dead)* — the sun-god who spent himself against the tide. His corpse is the moon; his heart-coal burns inside the Ember; his scattered cinders are the dragon-kind. He has no cult — you cannot pray to the dead — but he has *mourners*: the Moonwake, a lay devotion keeping lament-fires on high places at full moon. Doctrine holds he is not gone but *rejuvenating in death*, on the slow clock gods keep; the moon's phases are read as breathing. Whether the dragons are his heirs or merely his debris is deliberately unresolved (§14).

**OVET, the Door** *(dead and transformed)* — exists now only as Ulveth; yet the Free Reeds keep Ovet's old open-door rites at every threshold, and the Vaultwardens tolerate it uneasily. A theological time-bomb, deliberately planted: if Ovet's aspect could be *knotted back out* of Ulveth, death might become a door again — the explicit dream of at least two cults (§10) and the implicit stakes of the endgame.

**HEZRETH'SAVRA, the Shuttered Lantern** *(compound)* — the pantheon's great living knot: the Veiled One and the Lightbearer invoked as one being, "the light that is not seen — the lamp inside the dark inside the lamp." Born as a soldiers' devotion in the Long Dark (a hidden light survives; a shown light dies), formalized by Zolvah's rite, now patron of everyone who does bright work in dark places: rescue-diggers, plague-doctors, spies of good conscience, the midnight watch. The compound has its own temper — warmer than Hezreth, more secretive than Savra — and its own feast (the Shuttering, at winter solstice, when every lamp in the kingdom burns inside a closed box for one hour). Both constituent cults resent it slightly. It outranks neither. It is standing proof that the pantheon is a *grammar*, still generating new sentences.

> **Design note.** The pantheon deliberately has no evil god. Kadrash is necessary violence, Mureth necessary decay, Ithves is unpersoned entropy, and villainy is supplied by mortals, heretics, and the Fray. Destroying any god would break a load-bearing function — the design descends from the doctrine that the adversary must be lawful and the dark gods must have day jobs.

## 5. Cosmology & the Death System

### 5.1 The shape of the world

Suvareth is a raft, and cosmology is naval architecture. From top to bottom:

**The Loomside.** Not a place but a *side* — the underside of the Weave, where the gods have stood since the Hush. It cannot be traveled to, only listened through. Temples are thin spots. Dreams are thinner.

**The Raft** — the mortal world, floating on the Unsaid. By day the Ember crosses the sky; by night hang the moon (Khelsurat's cold corpse) and the stars, *the punctuation of the first sentence*.

**The Rimsea.** Toward the map's edges the Weave thins; the sea beyond the last charted water is Vesse wearing a sea's costume. Compasses forget north, sailors forget port, and ships that sail too far return crewed by people no records ever contained — or don't return. The rim is not a wall but a *gradient of forgetting*: the in-fiction floor under every map edge the world will ever ship.

**The Undervault.** Beneath everything, the body of Ulveth: twelve chambers descending, each the buried piece of a god, knotted on one cord. It is real and traversable — the deepest dungeons break into its upper halls, and every grave is a door onto its threshold. The living can walk in. Walking out is the hard part; the death system below is the law of that walk.

**Night is the Undervault's shadow cast upward.** Each of the twelve night-hours corresponds to the chamber the Lantern-barge is passing through; the dead are strongest when Savra is deepest. Midnight, when the Ember touches Ulveth's heart, is at once the most dangerous hour above ground and the one sacred moment when the boundary runs both ways — the canonical hour for resurrections, séances, and terrible mistakes. Dawn is not guaranteed; it is *delivered*.

### 5.2 The anatomy of a soul

A person is not a unit. A person is a *knot* — five strands tied at birth by Zolvah's rite into one working self. The strands, in the order the funeral liturgy names them:

| Strand | Vessic | What it is | What it needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Flesh | **suvet** | The body: your allotment of raft, the only strand that occupies a tile of the world | Food, warmth, and after death: burial, or at least honest rot |
| The Ember | **khelet** | The vital heat, a spark descended coal-to-coal from the First Flame; the strand that *wants* | Fuel — in life, food and fire; in death, offerings (this is why the dead are fed) |
| The Echo | **havra** | The mobile self: the strand that dreams, travels, and survives the body — a faint doubled image of you, Havir's tithe on every person | Anchorage — it must return regularly to its flesh (living: sleep; dead: the corpse or a relic) or it thins into a ghost |
| The Name | **nakh** | Your social existence: the strand that lives in other mouths and in the Rolls of Names | Mention. "The unmentioned name unravels" — recitation, inscription, memory |
| The Tally | **dekhat** | A small smooth stone that forms in the chest, layer by layer, recording everything you do; grows heavier with harm done, denser with duty kept; the strand the court reads | Nothing. The Tally needs nothing. The Tally is *for* someone else |

Death is the untying. The zol-knot fails and the strands scatter: the flesh falls where it stands; the ember gutters and clings to the flesh; the echo tears loose and drifts; the name stays with the living; and the tally sinks — straight down, through soil and stone, at the speed of grief, to wait for its owner on the shelf of the eleventh chamber. None of this is punishment. It is what knots do.

### 5.3 The Half-Passage: why death is usually not the end

Because Kadrash's stroke made death a *corridor* instead of a door, dying is not an event but a **journey** — and a journey can be turned around. A soul whose strands are recovered and re-knotted before it completes the twelve chambers has not "come back from death"; it simply *never finished dying*. The temples call this the Half-Passage, and a sacred economy exists to arrange it:

**The knot-shrines.** Zolvah's cord runs through every consecrated altar. A mortal who has spoken their name into a shrine's cord (the **binding-word**) has a registered anchor. When the knot fails, the echo is reeled, disoriented and diminished, to the last shrine holding its binding-word, where clergy perform the Re-knotting: a provisional body is granted (grown in the Silt Mothers' vat-gardens, §10), the ember re-lit from the shrine's lamp, and the person walks out alive, gasping, and *lessened*.

**The Toll.** Nothing crosses any part of the Undervault free — not even a soul turning back. The first gate takes its fee from what you carry in yourself: skill sinks toward ignorance, strength toward frailty; the gatekeepers have *eaten a share of your practice*. The Toll is proportional, lawful, non-negotiable — and **prepayable**: temples sell blessings, toll-credits inscribed on the strands in advance, each absorbing a share of the loss before the gate takes the rest. The poor buy one cheap blessing; the rich walk in quintuple-blessed. The liturgy is blunt: *the Door was murdered; passage services the Debt; your loss is a payment on a god's account.*

**The corpse.** The flesh does not travel. Where you fell, it lies — with everything it wore and carried, because objects are knotted to bodies, not echoes. For as long as it lasts, the corpse is the echo's *strongest lawful anchor*, and things left on it remain yours in the court's eyes for a term (custom: ten days; looters inside the term are cursed by every funerary code, which has never once stopped them). Recovering your own corpse — to reclaim goods, or to bury the flesh before the wild dead find it — is an ordinary, expected, dangerous errand of Suvarethi life: **the walk of shame and iron**.

**Grave-goods.** For the dead not turning back, goods are packed deliberately: lamp-oil for the dark chambers, toll-tokens for the gates, a name-card so the nakh travels with its owner, bread for the khelet, and above all the **passage-scroll** — a purchased manuscript of gate-names, guardian-names, and answers, workshop-copied with a blank left for the buyer's name, priced from a laborer's month to a noble's ransom. A well-equipped soul walks the corridor like a pilgrim; a pauper's like a beggar — which is why the minimal rite (a drop of river-water, one spoken name, a crumb) is doctrinally guaranteed *sufficient*, if not comfortable. Temples that forget this guarantee are the ones necromancers recruit outside.

### 5.4 The court at the heart: weighing and the Maw

A soul that completes the passage — by choice, by expiry of its anchors, or by the rare sentence of *final death* — descends to the eleventh hall, collects its tally-stone, and enters the twelfth: Ulveth's court, lit once a night by the visiting Ember.

The rite is the **Weighing**. Dekharu sits assessor. On one pan of the Level goes the tally-stone; on the other, the True Thread — a single thread of pure sereth, the weight of a life lived in pattern. The soul speaks the **Clean Account**: the recitation, before twelve-and-twelve chamber-wardens each addressed by name, of the harms *not* done. In-fiction scholarship argues, forever unresolved, whether the Weighing measures the life or the *preparation*: passage-scrolls sell warden-names and gag-clauses ("let my tally not testify against me"), and both the moral and the technical reading are attested in scripture. The canon refuses to settle it.

Balance, and the soul is spoken **savret** — "lit": re-knotted on the far side as a bright, effective, completed self, granted estate in the deep meadows of the twelfth chamber (a green country under a stone sky, watered by Sildra's oldest tears). The lit dead still *do things*: answer descendants' letters, guard family tombs, lean on the scales of the living world in small lawful ways. They are the setting's ancestor-infrastructure.

Imbalance, and the court does not rage. It *feeds*. Chained beside the Level lies the **Veskha**, the Unsaying Maw — a piece of the Fray Ulveth caught in the Long Dark and domesticated into an instrument of law: eyeless, patient, shaped like the fear of every animal at once. The failed tally goes to it, and with the tally, the knot's last hold. This is the **second death**: not punishment-forever, but *unsaying* — strands released back into the Unsaid, name struck from the Rolls. Nothing of the person continues to be. The liturgy's cold mercy: what the Maw takes was never sent anywhere worse; it was sent *nowhere*, which the wise fear more.

### 5.5 The broken cases

Every horror in the death-system is a strand-failure, and clergy diagnose the undead like physicians: **ghosts** (an echo without anchors — corpse destroyed, name forgotten; curable by re-anchoring); **wights and tomb-wardens** (lawful dead on legitimate duty — attacking one is a crime *against the court below*); **ghouls and rot-dead** (Mureth's sacrament interrupted, flesh puppeted on stolen ember-heat — necromancy's coin, §10–11); and worst, **the Frayed** — things partially unsaid, walking gaps in the Weave near the rim and the Heresy scars. Not undead. *Unfinished subtraction.*

> **Design note.** This section is the death loop of a grid RPG told as theology: shrine-binding = spawn registration; the Toll = death penalty, reducible by purchasable blessings; the corpse with a loot-timer = corpse-run and item risk; grave-goods and passage-scrolls = the consumable economy; the Weighing and Maw = the lore ceiling for true permadeath (story verdicts only, never random); night-hour danger scaling = the light cycle made mechanical; the lit dead = ancestor NPCs and any future player-legacy system.

## 6. Ages of the World

Suvarethi chroniclers count six ages. Dates are given in the scholarly convention **AS** (*After the Severing*) — negative numbers reach back into myth, where counting is confession of ignorance. The present year of the canon is **AS 3207**. Each age left strata: ruins that can be dated by their masonry, and creatures that can be dated by their ancestry. A learned traveler reads the world like a cut bank.

### 6.1 The Saying (before counting)

The age of creation: the First Mercy, the speaking of the Long Words, the weaving of the Weave, the making of the servant races and of humankind. No ruins date from the Saying — nothing had yet fallen down — but its *survivals* walk: the Gorai, the eldest knot-serpents, and the fae Unfinished, the age's living scrap-paper. **Stratum:** none (pre-architectural). **Creature legacy:** giants, fae, the primal spirits of river and hill.

### 6.2 The Open Age (ends at AS 0)

The long peace of the working Door: souls flowing out and in, gods walking openly, death a gentle dissolving. The chronicles of this age are the least trusted documents in the world — everyone who wrote them wrote from grief afterward. It ends in the Quarrel of the Door, the Severing, and the raising of the Undervault. **Stratum:** the *threshold ruins* — door-shaped megaliths on hilltops, all facing the sunset, older than any wall; the Free Reeds still hang offerings on them. **Creature legacy:** the oldest ghosts; the twelve *sever-stones* where pieces of Ovet first fell (now deep beneath later shrines); the sacred silt-eels, said to have drunk Sildra's first tears.

### 6.3 The Long Dark (c. AS 0–140)

The God-War: the vesk-tide against the named world, the gods fighting at full weight, Khelsurat's death, the forging of the Ember. A hundred and forty years without a true sun — mortals survived huddled at hearth-cults, and every culture's oldest songs are counting-songs from the dark ("name everyone in the house, that none be unsaid by morning"). **Stratum:** the *scars* — glass plains, hills folded like cloth, crater-lakes where a god stood in wrath; and the *cinderfalls*, where pieces of the burning First Flame struck. Scar-sites do not heal and cannot be built on; the Weave there is fused, not woven. **Creature legacy:** dragons (cinderfall-born, eldest first); the elemental kindreds — god-blood that never stopped obeying its last order; the Frayed remnants at the rim; stranded null-hounds.

### 6.4 The Withdrawal (c. AS 140–600)

The Hush: gods stepping behind the Weave, Nakhoro carrying the deep names beyond the rim, the covenant of absence. For mortals, an age of terrifying promotion — the keeping of the world handed to short-lived hands. The first temples rise (built to be *thin*, not grand), the Registry begins the Rolls of Names, and the servant races are discharged: the Gorai walk away from finished work, and the Vakhur legions are dismissed by a god who no longer takes the field — the most consequential demobilization in history, for the Vakhur were never taught what soldiers are *for* when there is no war. **Stratum:** first-temple foundations — cyclopean bases with mortarless seams no knife fits. **Creature legacy:** the Vakhur's feral drift begins; the first lawful tomb-wardens are commissioned; the savrim appear (doctrine: Savra's answer to mortals' new loneliness).

### 6.5 The Kingdoms (c. AS 600–2890)

Two thousand years of mortal history proper: riverine Sildarun under the Covenant Crowns, the Kadravai drum-confederacies, the timber kings of the Hovanmark, the delta league. Temple-economies mature into the world's banks; knot-theology flowers (Hezreth'Savra formalized c. AS 1450); the Undervault's upper chambers are mapped, courted, and legislated with. Kingship in Sildarun is sacral: the monarch is crowned *Suvan* — "the one who holds" — personally answerable for flood, harvest, and dawn-rites; a bad inundation is constitutional crisis. Dynasties rise and fall on temple certification, above all the Registry's power to enroll — or decline to enroll — an heir in the Rolls Royal. **Stratum:** most standing architecture — river-cities, terraced tombs, flood-temples, roads. **Creature legacy:** the bonded dead multiply with tomb-culture; cord-hags and hollow twins enter the record; dragons begin hoarding *names* as well as gold.

### 6.6 The Heresy Age (AS 2890–2921) — the Lantern King

The wound the present still limps on. **Suvakhelan IV**, called afterward **the Lantern King**, inherited the Sildarun high throne young, brilliant, and — the surviving fragments genuinely suggest — sincere. His grievance was real: the temple-banks certified his line, taxed his river, and had unmade his grandfather's chosen heir with a raised eyebrow. His revelation, recorded in his own hymn-cycle: *the gods are absent, but the Ember is present; the Ember is the only divine thing any eye can verify; therefore the Ember alone is God, and the rest is priestcraft.* He renamed himself **Khel'Suvan** — a mortal taking the divine glottal, the one blasphemy the naming-law had held in reserve since the Saying — and declared the **Khelat**: the doctrine of the Lone Ember.

Seventeen years followed: the pantheon's rites suspended, temple-estates seized into crown lamp-houses; the Registry's Rolls sequestered (not burned — *sequestered*; the King understood exactly what names were); the funerary pipeline stopped — no binding-words, no passage-scrolls, for the Khelat taught that souls need no corridor, they simply *return to the light*; erasure-gangs of illiterate soldiers chiseling the Long Words' names off ten centuries of stone, sparing Savra's emblem (she carried the Ember; the King read her as the Khelat's prophetess) and missing, everywhere, names they could not read; and a new capital, **Khelvassa**, Seat of the Ember, raised in nine years on the eastern scarps from standardized cast-stone blocks a child could lift, every street aligned to catch dawn like a gun-sight.

And the world began, quietly, to break — exactly as Law I promises. The rites were the pumps. Unbound souls thickened over the kingdom like weather; the wild dead rose in unprecedented numbers; the Fray crept inland at a dozen neglected thin-places — the erasure-gangs had been *feeding* it hacked-off names. In the last years came the Grey Plague, which the Khelat could not explain (its dead needed a corridor its doctrine denied); the King's own children died unbound; and the fragments from the palace's final winter are not hymns but letters — the King writing to a god he had made of a lamp, asking why it did not answer, unable to hear that the god the lamp burned for was dead before the founding of the world.

Suvakhelan IV died in AS 2919 — of grief, plague, or a Vaultwarden's long patience; the sources contradict each other with suspicious unanimity. The restoration was carried not by priests but by the river-army under the general **Dravessa**, who reopened the temples in the child-heir's name, recommissioned the funerary pipeline (three decades of backlogged dead: the Vaultwardens' *Great Processing* is its own epic), and let the Registry do to the Lantern King what he had done to the gods: name struck from the Rolls Royal, glottal unsaid by full liturgy, city left to the scarp winds. The chronicles call him only *the Lantern King* — a title, not a name, because to write the name would re-enroll it.

**Stratum:** unmistakable — cast-stone blocks, dawn-aligned axes, chiseled voids where god-names were, and the **Rolls-scars**: registry pages ritually blacked. Khelvassa stands mostly intact and wholly avoided, the largest ruin in the world (§9). **Creature legacy:** the *unbound* (three decades of corridor-denied dead, still being processed); the Frayed colonies of the eastern scarps; the erasure-golems built from sequestered Rolls; and the **Emberites**, the banned cult that keeps the Khelat alight in secret to this day (§10).

### 6.7 The Scarred Present (AS 2921–now; "now" = AS 3207)

Two hundred eighty-six years of aftermath. The temples restored, and restored *harder* — the post-Heresy priesthoods keep their mysteries classified, having learned what a king with theological confidence can do. The crown permanently weakened: every monarch since is crowned under the **Dravessan Concord**, requiring triple certification (Registry, Level, Vaultwardens). Personal piety flowered in the wreckage — the Heresy accidentally taught commoners that the gods could be *addressed directly*, and the folk shrines never gave that back. The Fray's inland fingers remain unfought in places; the Great Processing has a century of backlog left; Khelvassa waits on its scarp, and things live in it now; and somewhere the Emberites keep a lamp burning in a closed room, insisting — not entirely without a case, which is what makes them dangerous — that the Lantern King's only error was impatience.

**Stratum:** the present builds thin and pious, new work on Kingdoms bones. **Creature legacy:** everything above, all at once. This is the age the players inhabit: every era's monsters still in inventory, every era's ruins still open.

> **Design note.** The six ages are the content-dating system: every dungeon, ruin, and spawn should be assignable to one stratum, and mixed strata should mean something happened. The Heresy Age is the Amarna-transmutation — entrenched temple-banks → sincere absolutist king → one-god cutover → deleted death-infrastructure → collapse → military restoration → damnatio memoriae — with original divergences: the chosen god is a *lamp* that genuinely is not a god; the erasure fed the Fray; the corpse-backlog persists into the present as live content.

## 7. Peoples & Races

### 7.1 Humankind — the short words

Humans are the Speaker's last and favorite grammar: brief, plural, and alone among made things able to *say new sentences*. All human cultures share the five-strand soul, the shrine-binding habit, and the inherited commission ("you keep the keeping"); they differ in everything else.

**The Sildaruni** — the river kingdom's people (§9.1). Sedentary, literate, bureaucratic, devout in a contractual way: a Sildaruni prays the way one files paperwork with a superior one respects. Society runs on the flood calendar and the temple-banks; rank is measured in *enrollments* — how many Rolls carry your name. Virtue: continuity. Vice: certification-worship ("a thing not written has not quite happened"). Post-Heresy, a people with a scar where their pride was: it was *their* king who tried to unsay heaven. Player-flavor: the default civilization — priests, scribes, river-army soldiers, tomb-pilots.

**The Kadravai** — drum-confederacies of the southern desert (§9.2). Nomad herders and caravan-masters who hold that the world is a rhythm and settlements are missed beats. They keep the Drum Account, bury their dead standing (a standing soul walks the corridor faster), and are the only culture that *likes* Kadrash — the desert teaches his arithmetic early. Every Kadravai learns the **night-count**, the Long Dark counting-song; the third-syllable naming rite is a solo vigil against the dark. Virtue: endurance. Vice: contempt for walls that extends to the people behind them. Player-flavor: rangers, drum-priests, caravan guards, the world's best Fray-watchers.

**The Markfolk** — the Hovanmark's timber-and-iron highlanders (§9.3), organized in hearth-holds under barrow-kings. Their religion is the pantheon seen from below: less liturgy, more contract-with-the-local — the hill's spirit, the mine's dekhim, the barrow-ancestor who still answers if you bring the right beer. They add a third fold to the Twofold Burial: a lock of hair stays at the hearth, so every hold's fire is also a reliquary. Virtue: obligation kept over generations. Vice: feuds kept the same way. Player-flavor: fighters, smith-crafters, barrow-wards; closest to the giants and the dragon-hills.

**The Free Reeds** — the delta league (§9.4): boat-born traders and pilots who keep Ovet's open-door rites and regard inland theology as excellent business conducted over their heads. Governed by the **Moot of Masts**, one vote per hull. The world's sailors — the people who go nearest the Rim, whose charts, half map and half prayer, treat the gradient of forgetting as a navigational hazard with soundings. Virtue: openness. Vice: they will trade with anything that pays, and some things pay in names. Player-flavor: rogues, navigators, name-brokers, rim-divers.

### 7.2 The elder makes

**The Gorai** — the giants, Tekhur's scaffold-crew (Saying-stratum). Twelve to twenty feet, slow-spoken, geologically patient. They raised the highlands as formwork and were never decommissioned — merely left without instructions, which to a made-for-work being is a wound. Most sleep for decades in the high country ("waiting for the next order"); waking Gorai hire out as builders to whoever speaks *formally* — they physically cannot begin work without a properly phrased commission, a grammar-lock Tekhur built in. They do not worship; they consider themselves *evidence*. Hostile giants are almost always grief-cases: a Gorai whose work was demolished can go *foundation-mad*. Player-relation: NPC race — questgivers, gatekeepers, living archives of the Saying.

**The Vakhur** — the brute-folk, Kadrash's discharged legions (Withdrawal-stratum). Broad, tusked, clay-grey to rust-red, made from earth and shout to be soldiers — then dismissed by a god who stopped taking the field, their purpose amputated. Three thousand years later they are the world's tragedy-in-plain-sight: warbands that raid because a raid is the only liturgy they were ever taught, war-cries audibly corrupted Kadrashi rite. The canon keeps a door open: Vakhur are not *evil*, they are *unassigned*. Settled Vakhur exist — caravan guards, gate-cohorts, one famous monastery of Vakhur Paid-Edge monks — and the Order of the Paid Edge counts re-tasking them as penance-duty for its god's Debt. Player-relation: primary humanoid antagonist family *and* standing redemption-arc material.

**The Unfinished** — the fae: Nakhoro's discarded drafts (Saying-stratum). Sentences that crawled off the page incomplete, beautiful the way fragments are beautiful, each missing something load-bearing — a name, a shadow, a yes, a death. They haunt reed-margins, mirror-still water, and dusk (the seam between sayings). All fae hunger for completion, and mortal names are the completest things that will talk to them: their bargains, riddles, and thefts all orbit *acquiring names* — never give a fae your full name; a nickname is a copy, not the handle. They are lawful the way contracts are lawful: exact, remorseless, bound by their own wording. Courts: the **Reedmoot** (soft, riverine, curious) and the **Glasscourt** (dwelling in Long Dark scar-glass; cold, precise, collects last words). Player-relation: trickster antagonists, bargain-vendors, the world's strangest quests.

**The Molded** — the freed constructs (Kingdoms-stratum, multiplied in the Heresy). Bodies of fired clay, cast stone, or bundled reed, animated by Registry-script: a name-scroll where a heart would be. Built as tomb-servants and flood-workers — then the Lantern King's scribes mass-produced golems from *stolen* enrolled names, and some of those names woke up. The post-Heresy **Suvet Ruling** (AS 2934) created the petition-name system: a Molded who can demonstrate *wanting* before the House of the Level is enrolled as a person, one name-root at a time. Perhaps four thousand personed Molded exist; unpersoned ones remain property, and the line between is the setting's sharpest ethical edge. They cannot shrine-bind (nothing knotted them; their death is final), which makes personed Molded the world's most careful people. Player-relation: rare NPC citizens, tomb "constructs" with a moral trapdoor under every fight, and the question the Registry cannot close: *is a spoken name a soul?*

### 7.3 The between-folk

**The Marrowless** — humans born in Fray-touched country: outwardly ordinary, but the Weave holds them loosely — thin shadows, dogs ignore them, shrine-cords accept their binding-words reluctantly (their Toll runs lighter: the gates find less of them to eat). Feared unfairly; organized into quiet brotherhoods; over-represented among rim-sailors and Vault-guides, where being hard to notice is a wage.

**The Emberkin** — bloodlines from Long Dark hearth-cults that sheltered cinderfall for generations. Warm-handed, fire-tolerant, prone to draconic attention (dragons treat them as distant, disappointing relatives). The Heresy recruited them heavily — the Khelat flattered their ancestry — and the present age has not finished forgiving them.

> **Design note.** Cultures are built to be class-flavor and reputation-faction at once; the elder makes supply the three classic fantasy antagonist families (brute, fae, construct) each with a mythological wound instead of an alignment; and every race answers to a god or an age from §4/§6, per Law III.

## 8. Creature Families — Bestiary Seeds

Sixteen families. Each entry: origin → temperament → example creatures (low/mid/high tier). Per Law III, no creature may be added outside a family, and no family without an origin in §3–§6.

**1. Dragons — cinders of the dead sun** *(Long Dark)*. Fragments of burning Khelsurat that fell, cooled unevenly, and remembered pride. They hoard what preserves memory — gold, names, heat — because a fully forgotten cinder goes out. They despise the Ember ("the lamp wearing our father's heart") but cannot approach it. *Examples:* **ash-drake** (low: dog-sized cinder, all appetite), **kiln-wyrm** (mid: tunnel-melting hoarder of mine-country), **Vessakhel the Twice-Cooled** (high: an elder cinder that has died and reignited once already; speaks; collects the true names of extinct things).

**2. The Vakhur — discharged legions** *(Withdrawal)*. Kadrash's clay-and-shout soldiery, unassigned for three millennia (§7.2); warbands run on corrupted drill-liturgy, their "shamans" sergeants who misremember loudest. *Examples:* **vakh levy** (low), **drill-caller** (mid: chant-buffs its band — silence it first), **Last-Sergeant Gorvakk** (high: a Withdrawal-era original, still executing a three-thousand-year-old rearguard order no one ever countermanded).

**3. The Unfinished — fae drafts** *(Saying)*. Nakhoro's discarded phrasings (§7.2). All fae combat is contract-shaped: they cannot break stated rules, only exploit them. *Examples:* **reed-wisp** (low: lantern-mimic that leads travelers off causeways), **nickname-thief** (mid: wears every alias you have ever used, one per turn), **the Glasscourt Envoy** (high: a duel of exact wording; kills with your own quoted sentences).

**4. The lawful dead — the court's servants** *(Open Age onward)*. Bonded echoes and lit souls on legitimate duty: tomb-wardens, Vault debt-collectors, funerary escorts. Fighting them is legal trespass with paperwork consequences. *Examples:* **seal-wight** (low: animate burial-writ), **procession-knight** (mid: fights only between graveside and gate), **the Eleventh-Chamber Bailiff** (high: serves summonses that *bind*).

**5. The wild dead — strand-failures** *(all ages; surged in the Heresy)*. Ghosts, drowned unmourned, corridor-denied plague-dead. Not evil; *broken* — every wild dead has a diagnosable missing strand, and re-anchoring (name-restoration, burial, mention) is always a valid alternative to combat. *Examples:* **fen-shade** (low), **unbound wailer** (mid: still queueing for the Great Processing), **the Grey Bride** (high: a name-erased noblewoman who un-names those she touches).

**6. The stolen dead — necromancy's coin** *(Kingdoms onward)*. Mureth's rot arrested and puppeted: ghouls, stitched labor-corpses, bone-engines — each a theft from the Gleaner's sacrament and from a soul's flesh-anchor. *Examples:* **field ghoul** (low), **charnel foreman** (mid: raises its own workgang), **the Composted King** (high: layered from forty enrolled corpses; the Registry wants it *unraveled*, not destroyed).

**7. Elementals — god-blood in uniform** *(Long Dark)*. Where gods bled in the war, the droplets kept obeying their last order: not nature-spirits but *unrelieved sentries*. Speak the correct stand-down phrasing (always a research quest) and they simply stop. *Examples:* **ember-sprite** (low: Khelsurat's blood, still attacking the dark), **levee-brute** (mid: Sildra's blood, drowning "invaders" at old battle-fords), **the Unrelieved Pillar** (high: Tekhur's blood holding up a sky that no longer needs holding; the land around it cannot be built on until it is talked down).

**8. The Gorai and their works — foundation-folk** *(Saying)*. Sleeping giants, waking builders, grief-cases (§7.2), plus their animate works. *Examples:* **hill-sleeper** (mid: dangerous only if woken rudely), **jointless watcher** (mid: mortarless masonry that objects to your presence), **foundation-mad mason** (high: rebuilding a demolished temple with whatever and whoever is nearby).

**9. The Frayed — walking subtraction** *(Long Dark rim; Heresy scars)*. Things partially unsaid: a wolf with no sound, a bear you *cannot* fear (which gets people killed), landscapes that forget you into them. They drop nothing; they *leave gaps*. *Examples:* **null-hound** (low: absence with teeth), **the ungrazed field** (mid: an ambulatory patch of forgetting), **Hole-in-the-Shepherd** (high: a person-shaped absence that recruits by erasure; the region's shepherds all remember him, none can say why).

**10. Cinder-beasts — the dragons' spillage** *(Long Dark)*. Lesser cinderfall in living shapes; Emberkin can calm them, everyone else runs. *Examples:* **ash-owl** (low), **pyre-jackal pack** (mid), **the Standing Bonfire** (high: a cinder "keeping the hearth" of a village square for three thousand years, feeding it whatever comes).

**11. River-kin — Sildra's household** *(Saying/Open Age)*. Mostly benign; lethal when funerary custom is violated nearby (they enforce the ten-day term). *Examples:* **grief-heron** (low: mimics mourners' voices), **silt-eel matriarch** (mid: funeral-barge escort; attacks river-grave looters), **the First Ferry** (high: a barge-spirit that accepts exactly one passenger a night, no questions, either direction — a standing plot device).

**12. Oath-fauna — Zolvah's and Dekharu's small servants** *(Withdrawal onward)*. Civilization's ambient enforcement layer. *Examples:* **oath-wasp swarm** (low: stings only perjurers), **dekhim gate-sphinx** (mid: no riddles, just *knows*), **the Grown Mouse** (high: fed three centuries of a city's broken promises; now the size of the granary it lives under).

**13. Echo-spawn — Havir's crooked returns** *(all ages)*. Everything that comes back *almost* right; clusters at reflective water and anywhere someone famous lied. *Examples:* **mockingbeast** (low: fights you with your own last move), **mirror-mite infestation** (mid: your reflection lags, then leaves), **a Hollow Twin** (high: your build, your gear, your face; it wants your zol-knot, because it never got one).

**14. Vault-fauna — the Undervault's natives** *(Open Age)*. Not dead, not alive: creatures evolved inside a god's body. *Examples:* **corridor-lamprey** (low: drinks Toll-payments mid-transit), **gate-hound of the Fourth Chamber** (mid: lawful; bribable with grave-bread), **the Shelf-Warden** (high: guards the tally-shelf; has never lost one; would like to keep it that way).

**15. Heresy-remnants — the Lantern King's leavings** *(Heresy Age)*. *Examples:* **cast-stone eraser** (mid: chisels names off *you* — degrades titles and reputation until repaired), **lamp-wight** (mid: a soul that "returned to the light" and found it was only a lamp; burns cold; worships any light it sees), **the Un-Signed** (high: the Heresy's chief scribe, self-erased to escape judgment; unkillable because officially nonexistent — the quest is to *re-enroll* him so he can die).

**16. Rim-things — the gradient's fauna** *(outside the ages)*. What sails in off the Rimsea when the charts run out. *Examples:* **manifest-crab** (low: shell of cargo papers), **the Complement** (mid: eleven sailors who were never born, looking for their twelfth), **Landfall** (high: a thing that thinks it is an island; periodically "discovered"; the discovery parties become its flora).

> **Design note.** Families 1–3 carry the classic fantasy surface (dragon/orc/fae); 4–6 split "undead" into three mechanically and morally distinct economies (lawful/broken/stolen) — the death-system doing bestiary work; 9 and 16 give the world horror at its edges without a devil; 12 and 15 make law and history spawnable. Every family names its stratum, so spawn placement is world-dating (Law III).

## 9. Regions & Geography

Suvareth's charted world is one great landmass shaped like a spread hand, palm to the south, fingers to the north, with the river Sil running down its lifeline. Seven regions, ordered river-first.

### 9.1 Sildarun — the River Kingdom

The palm of the world: a thousand miles of the Sil's floodplain, black-soiled, canal-veined, terrace-tombed. The river *is* the god's grief-road, and the kingdom is one long act of accompaniment: every city fronts the water with a funerary quay, every field is planted over composted ancestors, and the yearly flood — Sildra walking her mourning again — resets fertility and tax rolls together. Capital: **Sur-Sildra**, the Gathered City, on the river's one great island, where the Covenant Crowns are kept and the three certifying temples stand in a triangle around the palace — post-Heresy architecture: the crown is *inside* the temples' sightlines, on purpose. The kingdom's rhythm: inundation, sowing, harvest, and the Processional Month, when the year's dead are barged downstream in convoy. **Tone:** breadbasket, bureaucracy, piety, tombs. **Strata:** Kingdoms everywhere, Heresy-voids on older walls. **Threats:** field ghouls in the charnel-gardens, grief-herons, river-grave looters, temple-bank intrigue, and beneath the oldest tombs, doors into the Undervault's first chamber.

### 9.2 The Kadra Reach — the Red-Drum Desert

South of the palm, rust-red barrens under an enormous sky. The Reach is not chaos — the liturgy is insistent — it is *orderly anti-life*: predictable, patient, honest about its terms, the landscape Kadrash's arithmetic loves best. The Kadravai run its caravan-roads drum-tower to drum-tower (relay-percussion outruns horses), herd between bone-white oases, and stand the southern Fray-watch. In the deep interior lies the **Anvil Glass**, a god-scar where something stood and fought until the sand vitrified for thirty miles — the Glasscourt fae dwell in it, cinder-beasts den along its rim. **Tone:** endurance, honor-culture, wide horizons, buried things. **Strata:** Long Dark scars, standing-burial fields, threshold megaliths on the far rim. **Threats:** pyre-jackals, vakh warbands, glass-fae bargains, thirst.

### 9.3 The Hovanmark — the Highland Formwork

The fingers of the hand: cold parallel ranges the Gorai raised as sky-formwork, now the Markfolk's timber-and-iron country. Terraced holds under barrow-crowned hills; mine-mouths with dekhim at the gates; hot springs where a giant's forge-blood ran. The high passes host sleeping Gorai — marked, fenced, *respected* — and the deep ranges are dragon-country: kiln-wyrms in the coal seams, and on the highest cinder-peak an elder dragon to whom the Markfolk pay a negotiated tithe of remembered names (bards perform *at* the mountain; it is cheaper than fire). **Tone:** northern fantasy proper — halls, feuds, barrows, mines — wired into the engine underneath (the barrow-dead are lit souls; the feud-law is Dekharu's). **Strata:** Saying-survivals, first-temple cyclopean bases, Kingdoms mine-works. **Threats:** kiln-wyrms, foundation-mad Gorai, barrow-wights where feud-law broke, avalanche-elementals still holding a Long Dark front line.

### 9.4 The Ovessin — the Delta of Doors

Where the Sil unbinds into a hundred channels and meets the sea: reed-cities on stilts, the Moot of Masts, salt-gardens, and more threshold-megaliths than anywhere in the world — the Open Age's door-stones, which the Free Reeds garland and feed. The delta is the kingdom's lung and its loophole: goods, heresies, and refugees all enter here. In the outer marches the channels braid into the **Whisper Roads**, waterways that don't stay mapped, where the Reedmoot holds its soft court and the Rim-gradient begins. **Tone:** trade, mist, negotiation, beautiful wrongness at the edges. **Strata:** Open Age megaliths, Kingdoms port-works. **Threats:** reed-wisps, nickname-thieves, manifest-crabs, name-brokers selling what should not be sold, drowned unmourned after every storm.

### 9.5 The Ember Scarps — the Heresy's Country

East of the river the land steps up in dry shelves toward the sunrise — and on the third shelf stands **Khelvassa**, the Lantern King's dawn-aligned capital: nine years in the building, two hundred eighty-six in the abandoning, the largest and best-preserved ruin in the world. Cast-stone streets sighted on the equinox sunrise; the Lamp-House palace with its thousand cold sconces; the sequestration-archives; and everywhere the chiseled voids where god-names were, which the Frayed wear like doorways. Lamp-wights keep the dead city's watch; erasure-golems patrol orders no one can countermand — their countermand *was written in the Rolls the King sequestered*, and recovering it is a canonical epic; in the deep archive the Un-Signed keeps his unbearable office. The scarps around are Marrowless country. **Tone:** the setting's great dungeon-region — silence, dawn-light, guilt. **Strata:** Heresy Age, almost pure. **Threats:** bestiary family 15, Frayed colonies, Emberite pilgrims who do not want witnesses.

### 9.6 The Veskrim Coast — the Gradient

The western, outermost shore, where the Weave visibly thins: fishing towns whose horizons sometimes show ships that never arrive; lighthouses run by the Vigilant as *literal* Weave-anchors (each is a consecrated thin-place pump; a dark lighthouse is a regional emergency); beyond the last light, the Rimsea. The coast folk are rim-hardened: Marrowless brotherhoods, rim-divers salvaging cargo from ships that un-happened, and the Watch of the Last Light holding the thin-places the Heresy left unfought. **Tone:** coastal horror, duty at the edge of definition. **Strata:** none — the Rim predates and outlasts the ages. **Threats:** null-hounds, the Complement, unsaid-wraiths, the rim-cults' standing temptation — and the sea itself, which forgets swimmers.

### 9.7 The Undervault — the World Below

Listed with the regions because it *is* one: the twelve-chambered body of the buried god, reachable through every deep tomb, some caves, and the great funerary gates beneath Sur-Sildra. The upper chambers (First–Fourth) are almost municipal: toll-gates, waiting-halls, the lawful dead's barracks, Vaultwarden embassies — living pilgrims with permits are routine. The middle chambers (Fifth–Ninth) are wilderness: vault-fauna, unprocessed Heresy backlog, necromancer poaching camps, and the nightly passage of the Lantern-barge, which every sane traveler shelters from (the light is not for the living; Ithves follows it). The deep chambers (Tenth–Twelfth) are the court: the tally-shelf, the Level, the Maw, the deep meadows of the lit dead — open to the living only by summons, and the summons is never good news. **Tone:** the underworld as infrastructure — half cathedral, half customs house, all the way down. **Threats and wonders:** families 4, 5, 6, 14; the Debt's arithmetic made architecture; and at the very bottom, once a night, the one place where the light and the deep still keep an appointment.

> **Design note.** Hub-and-spoke geography: Sildarun as starter hub (river = road), Kadra Reach and Hovanmark as the two classic biome wings, Ovessin as trade-and-trickery hub, Ember Scarps as flagship dungeon region and Heresy wound, Veskrim as endgame horror frontier, Undervault as the vertical region everything connects to (every tomb is a dungeon entrance by cosmological fiat). Two regions — the Scarps and the Rim — are wounds, one historical, one cosmological.

## 10. Factions, Cults & the Temple Economy

### 10.1 The temple economy: how the world banks

Suvareth mints little coin. The realm's real currency is **enrollment** — recorded claims on future flows: harvest-shares, toll-shares, labor-days, plunder-tithes. The temples are the ledger-keepers, and therefore the banks:

- Every temple of scale runs three rooms: a **granary** (deposits in kind), a **roll-room** (enrollment of assignments: who is owed what share of which flow), and a **rite-floor** (what makes it *binding* — a contract sworn on Zolvah's cord and weighed at Dekharu's level is enforced by oath-wasps and afterlife audit, not bailiffs alone).
- An office — tax-assessor, lock-keeper, tomb-pilot — comes bundled with named revenue streams and named duties. Wealth is a *portfolio of assignments*; theft at scale is forgery of enrollment papyri, and the greatest heist stories are ledger-crimes.
- War feeds the system: plunder is a lawful, tithed revenue class, and every victorious campaign pours a god's share into that god's temples — which is how the temples became richer than the crown, which is how the Heresy became thinkable. The Dravessan Concord now caps temple landholding; the temples honor the cap the way water honors a sieve.

The **funerary pipeline** is the economy's deepest artery: binding-words, blessings, passage-scrolls, provisional bodies, tomb-endowments (perpetual offerings funded by land-shares — which can *default*, orphaning the dead they feed), and mortuary insurance guilds for the poor. Death is Suvareth's largest industry. Everyone resents this. No one has a better idea. The Lantern King had a better idea, and the kingdom is still processing his results.

### 10.2 The great temples (the licensed powers)

- **The Registry (Nakhoro).** Keeper of the Rolls of Names; licenser of all name-magic; certifier of births, deaths, personhood, and royal heirs. The Registry never fights — it *declines to enroll*. Internal politics: the Blackpage Question — restore, destroy, or *keep studying* the Heresy's sequestered Rolls; the studying faction is winning, and that should worry everyone.
- **The Vaultwardens (Ulveth).** The funerary church-bank: embalmer-priests, corridor-lawyers, blessing-vendors, the Great Processing's exhausted civil service. Their power is monopoly on lawful passage; their scandal is tiered service (doctrine says the minimal rite suffices; the fee schedule says otherwise).
- **The House of the Level (Dekharu).** Magistrates, standards-keepers, contract-registrars: small, incorruptible, structurally load-bearing. A Level priest who lies once is expelled *retroactively* — every document they ever sealed must be re-weighed, which has bankrupted provinces.
- **The Silt Mothers (Sildra).** Midwife-embalmers, flood-almanac keepers, growers of provisional bodies in the vat-gardens of Sur-Sildra. The most beloved temple, the least wealthy by choice; their lament-singers are the kingdom's emergency healers.
- **The Kilnwrights (Tekhur), the Vigilant (Savra), the Order of the Paid Edge (Kadrash), the Gleaners (Mureth), the Drawn Curtain (Hezreth), the cord-registrars of Zolvah** — each as sketched in §4, each holding one civic monopoly (construction, night-watch, war-blessing, charnel-gardens, confession, oaths), each politicking accordingly.
- **The Shuttered Lantern (Hezreth'Savra).** The compound cult: small, elite, doctrinally awkward — living proof that mortal liturgy can *make new gods by knotting*, which every establishment temple has excellent reasons to avoid discussing.

### 10.3 The banned and the buried

**The Emberites — the Khelat surviving.** The Lantern King's faith, two hundred eighty-six years underground: cells that keep a lamp burning in a closed room, teach that the pantheon is priestcraft over one verifiable light, and await the **Rekindled King**, a prophesied ruler who will finish the work *patiently* this time. Their strength: the Khelat's central experience — *I have seen the light; I need no broker* — is genuinely available to anyone at dawn, and the temple-banks genuinely are everything the Heresy said they were. Their horror: the radical cells have concluded the Ember is caged divinity, and that *freeing* it — extinguishing the lamp so the "true sun" can return — is the great liberation. They do not know what the reader knows: there is no true sun to return. Status: hunted by the Vigilant, infiltrated by the Drawn Curtain, romanticized by students everywhere.

**The rim-cults — the Unsaying.** Coastal covens that feed names to the gradient: first their own regrets, then their enemies', then whatever the whisper asks. Some are grief-cases (unsaying a memory really does stop it hurting); the inner circles hold that the Raft was the error and the Unsaid is home — Ithves is their *psalm*, not their god (you do not worship a mouth; you fill it). The one faction with which no dialogue is canonically possible.

**The necromantic counterculture — the Free Gleaning.** Not a church: a trade network of hedge-necromancers, corpse-brokers, and unlicensed resurrectionists, thickest wherever the funerary pipeline's price gouging is worst. Their case is uncomfortably strong: the Vaultwardens sell the corridor by the yard; the Free Gleaning offers the poor a *working* afterlife on credit — your body labors after death to pay your family's debts (stitched labor-corpses staff half the delta's worst docks through intermediaries). Their crime is precise, per Mureth's theology: arrested rot, stolen ember-heat, flesh-anchors puppeted so the echo cannot rest. The Gleaners hunt them with sad thoroughness — it is *their* god being robbed — yet village opinion often shelters them, and the smartest Vaultwardens read the Free Gleaning as a price signal wearing a skull mask.

### 10.4 The martial and the civil

- **The River-Army of Sildarun.** The Dravessan legacy: the force that ended the Heresy and knows it. Constitutionally kingmaker; institutionally allergic to theology ("we restored the gods; a second time would be a habit").
- **The Watch of the Last Light.** The joint temple garrison of the Veskrim lighthouses. Chronically underfunded because its enemy produces no plunder — you cannot tithe a share of *nothing*.
- **The Drum-Confederacies.** The Kadravai's mobile polity: caravan-law, relay-towers, the southern Fray-watch — run on honor-debt instead of enrollment; the two economies' friction is a permanent trade-quest engine.
- **The Moot of Masts.** The delta league's hull-vote parliament: pilots, salvage law, rim-charts, and a deliberately vague policy on name-brokerage that everyone upriver finds enraging.
- **The Paid Edge's Vakhur Mission.** The war-temple's penance-project: re-tasking and monastically retraining brute-folk warbands. Forty years old; three monasteries; one catastrophic failure nobody discusses; slowly working.

> **Design note.** Factions map to reputation tracks: three licensed super-temples (Registry, Vaultwardens, Level) form the establishment triangle; the crown and river-army form the secular counterweight; Emberites/rim-cults/Free Gleaning are the three flavors of forbidden alignment (heresy, horror, crime), each with a real grievance so player sympathy is a choice, not a trick. The temple-bank mechanics (enrollment portfolios, tithed plunder, endowment default) are the loot-and-economy fiction, and the funerary pipeline is the death-service vendor layer of §5 wearing its institutional face.

## 11. The Magic System — Sayable Work

### 11.1 What casting is

Magic in Suvareth is **quotation**. The world is a spoken sentence still being said; a caster borrows its grammar and inserts a clause. The art is called **sayable work** (*nakh-tekh*, "name-shaping"), and its practice follows from four in-fiction facts:

1. **The Weave parses.** Reality executes well-formed utterances in Vessic's deep register. A spell is not a request; it is a *valid sentence* the world cannot refuse to run.
2. **Grammar is load.** Every inserted clause borrows tension from the surrounding Weave. Small sayings ripple; great sayings *pull*, and the Weave must re-tension afterward — felt by the caster as exhaustion and by the world, if abused, as fraying.
3. **Names are handles.** To affect a thing directly, address it. Common names give a grip; true names give *command*. Every entity's resistance is, formally, a dispute over how well you have named it.
4. **The gods are precedent.** All mortal spells are descended quotations — Sildra's laments (all healing), Kadrash's field-orders (severing and warding), Tekhur's work-chants (shaping), Havir's returns (illusion), Savra's vigil-calls (light), Zolvah's knots (binding). Casting is citation, and the license to cite is what temples actually teach.

### 11.2 Why spells have words, runes, and costs

**Spoken words** are live execution: fast, flexible, loud. Combat verbs are two-to-four syllable imperative clauses — a battle-caster sounds like someone giving orders to the air, because that is precisely what is happening. Design mapping: fixed incantations, learned as items of knowledge, castable only when known.

**Runes** are *compiled* speech: a saying inscribed onto a bearer (stone, bone, paper, blade) with the load pre-paid at inscription. A rune is a spell that already happened, waiting for its trigger — which is why runes can be stockpiled, traded, and used by people who could never speak the sentence themselves. Rune-craft is Registry-licensed; unlicensed runes are the setting's gunrunning.

**Cooldown and fatigue** are Weave re-tension. After a great saying the local grammar is *taut*: repeating the clause too soon finds the sentence "already occupied." Mana and cooldowns are the same physics at two scales. Deep-register mana is called **breath** (*hev*); the folk idiom for an exhausted caster — "out of breath" — is theologically exact.

**Components and foci** are grammatical objects: a saying *about* fire lands better holding an ember; a healing chanted over river-water cites Sildra's precedent in material form. Foci are optional but discount the load — grammar prefers demonstratives.

**The casting registers** run from prayer (a request routed to a god's thread: slow, cheap, requires standing) through licensed citation (temple-taught spellcraft: the adventurer's toolkit) to **raw saying** — original composition in the deep register, the rarest art, feared because an ill-formed original sentence does not fizzle: it *runs wrong*, executing what you said instead of what you meant. Every horror in the "wizard's tower" genre of folklore is a syntax error still executing.

### 11.3 The name economy

True names are keys, so the world treats them like plutonium. Registered mortals have their deep-register name held in Registry vaults (this is what enrollment *is*); speaking a person's true name gives targeting, command fragments, and — with sustained liturgy — editing. Hence nickname culture, fae paranoia, delta name-brokers, dragons hoarding the names of extinct things, and the gods' own deep names removed beyond the rim — the single divine act that made the Hush safe. Erasure is the dark mirror: chisel a name from enough records and mentions and the referent loses purchase on reality — slowly for people (the Grey Bride), instantly for anything that was *only* a name (erasure-golems). The Heresy weaponized erasure at kingdom scale and accidentally fed the Fray; it has been the Registry's tightest-controlled rite ever since. **Pun-law:** words that share sound share essence in the deep register — homophony is a real physical coupling, which makes poets dangerous, puns structurally significant, and certain rhymes illegal.

### 11.4 What necromancy violates

Necromancy is not "dark magic" — the deep register has no dark verbs. It is **grammatical theft**, and the theology is precise about the three counts:

1. **Theft from Mureth:** arresting rot suspends the turning-under; the matter never reaches the soil-cycle; the sacrament is interrupted mid-word.
2. **Theft from the corpse's owner:** the flesh is the echo's lawful anchor; puppeting it evicts the tenant. The wild dead around necromantic sites are not side effects — they are the *evicted*.
3. **Theft of ember-heat:** animation requires vital spark, and a corpse has none; it must be siphoned — from the necromancer (self-consuming), from livestock (detectable), or from victims (the capital form).

What necromancy is *not*: speaking with the dead (lawful, licensed, and a sacrament — the lit dead answer letters); commanding bonded tomb-wardens (lawful contract); resurrection through the Half-Passage (the system working as designed). The line is anchors and consent, not squeamishness — a licensed Vaultwarden animating a consenting debtor's corpse under seal is *legal* (the Free Gleaning's whole gray market is arbitrage on exactly this clause). The unforgivable act, above even necromancy, is **feeding the Fray**: deliberate unsaying, the rim-cults' rite. Necromancy steals from the system; unsaying deletes the system. The first gets you hanged. The second gets your name pre-erased before the trial, so that whatever is hanged is already no one.

> **Design note.** This section rationalizes the standard grid-RPG kit without naming it: fixed spell-incantations (valid sentences), rune items crafted in advance and usable by non-casters (compiled speech), mana + cooldown (breath + Weave re-tension), spell-licenses as trainer-gating (citation rights), component discounts (grammatical objects), and a hard lore floor under the necromancy-antagonist economy. The pun-law clause is deliberate soil for riddle content and discovery mechanics.

## 12. Hero Legends & Artifacts

Seven legends, each seeding quest-chains and unique items. Legends are canon *as legends*: the events are disputed in-world, the artifacts are not.

### 12.1 Dravessa and the Unread Order

The general who ended the Heresy never fought a battle for it. Met at Khelvassa's gates by the King's erasure-golems, she read aloud, from memory, the enrollment-clause of every soldier conscripted into their making — and the golems' stolen names *recognized themselves*, and the constructs knelt. She had spent nine years memorizing sequestered Rolls smuggled out by Registry clerks; the army calls it the cheapest victory ever won and holds an annual feast of Reading Aloud. **Artifact: the Dravessan Satchel** — a dispatch-bag that preserves any document against erasure, fire, and time. Currently missing; every faction in §10 wants it first. **Quest seeds:** the satchel-hunt; a surviving kneeling-golem seeking the clause that freed it; the feast interrupted by an Emberite cell.

### 12.2 Ossel Threewake and the Chart of What Isn't

The delta's great rim-pilot sailed past the last lighthouse *three times* and returned with her name intact — a feat no other navigator has managed once. Her method: a hide chart annotated entirely in negations ("no bottom here," "no north here," "here the crew you remember is not the crew you have"). Her third voyage brought back the Complement's twelfth sailor, alive and impossible; her fourth is why there is no fourth legend. **Artifact: the Chart of What Isn't** — held by the Moot of Masts under triple lock; grants safe rim-passage at the cost of one true memory per league, *chosen by the chart*. **Quest seeds:** escorting a Registry auditor cataloguing the chart; the twelfth sailor, now old, asking to be returned; a rim-cult scheme to annotate a coastal town out of existence.

### 12.3 The Twice-Woven Priest

A Silt Mother of the plague years — name unrecorded at her own request, which the Registry granted once and never again — performed the only unlicensed re-knotting ever to succeed: she rebuilt a dead child's zol-knot from her own strands, giving up two of her five. She lived on with no echo and no tally — unable to dream, impossible to judge: at her death the court found nothing to weigh, and Ulveth's ruling is the shortest in the Rolls: "Paid elsewhere." The child grew up ordinary, which the liturgy insists is the point. **Artifact: the Threadless Shawl** — lets a dying wearer transfer one strand to another person, once, destroying the shawl. It resurfaces every few generations, always in a poor district, always just in time. **Quest seeds:** the shawl surfacing in an epidemic; a necromancer hunting the "judgeless" grave for a corpse with no legal owner; the child's descendants, who all cast thin shadows.

### 12.4 Gorvan Hollow-Anvil, the Giant Who Finished

The only Gorai to complete his commission ("build until the sky needs no holding"). Every other giant waits; Gorvan *decided the clause had completed*, laid down his tools on a Hovanmark summit, and became the first Gorai to die of finishedness. His body is the mountain **Hollow-Anvil**; his decision is the legend — the Markfolk cite him at every retirement, treaty, and deathbed: the courage to declare a thing done. **Artifact: the Downed Hammer** — a maul no living creature has lifted; it will rise for whoever can state, in the deep register, a work that is truly finished. The Kilnwrights have a nine-century waiting list of attempts. **Quest seeds:** a foundation-mad giant curable only by Gorvan's precedent; an erasure-golem that walked to Hollow-Anvil and has waited, off-order, for centuries; the yearly attempt-festival, and the year the hammer moves.

### 12.5 The Lantern King's Last Letter

The final letter Suvakhelan IV wrote to the Ember, sealed and never sent — because, the legend says, he addressed it at the end not to the lamp but to *whoever carries it*, and understood in the final line who that was, and what she had been doing every night of his reign, and what that meant about everything he had torn down. The letter's existence is confirmed; its text is not. The Vaultwardens hold that Savra received it anyway — everything truly addressed arrives — and that its answer is why Emberite lamps sometimes gutter in perfect calm. **Artifact: the letter itself** — location unknown; Khelvassa's deep archive is the perennial rumor. Reading it is prophesied to either end the Emberite heresy or complete it; no version says which. **Quest seeds:** the flagship archive-delve; an Emberite schism between cells that want it read and cells that want it burned; a Drawn Curtain confessor who may already know the text and can, of course, never say.

### 12.6 Kammat of the Ninth Hour

A Kadravai drum-priest who walked into the Undervault *alive* to contest her brother's Weighing — he died mid-oath, tally imbalanced by a promise he never got to keep — arguing before the Level that an interrupted oath is Kadrash's wound like any other death, and belongs on the Debt. The argument *balanced*. Her brother passed lit; the ruling entered the deep precedents ("the Kammat Clause"); and Kadrash's account grew by the only Debt-line ever added *in mercy* — his own war-temple celebrates it. Kammat drummed the whole way down and back; vault-fauna still make way for her rhythm. **Artifact: the Ninth-Hour Drum** — safe conduct through the middle chambers for a party that keeps the rhythm unbroken; miss a beat and it lapses *immediately*. **Quest seeds:** learning the rhythm (a skill-chain, not a fetch); a Free Gleaning crew abusing drummed conduct for poaching; a dead ally whose Weighing can be contested under the Kammat Clause — the canonical resurrection-epic.

### 12.7 The Un-Signed's Apprentice

The Heresy's chief scribe erased himself to dodge judgment (§8, family 15) — but his apprentice, a Registry clerk named **Nakhotess**, spent her life re-writing him: interviewing everyone who remembered him, forging nothing, building the mention-web that will one day re-enroll him whether he consents or not. She died with the work unfinished; her scattered notebooks, the **Nakhotess Folios**, each make the Un-Signed *slightly more killable* — and more visible, to himself most of all. The sting: her notes reveal he was kind once, and erased himself in shame rather than cunning — and the Registry has ruled this changes nothing, because the Rolls do not enroll motives. **Artifact: the Folios** — eleven recoverable; the twelfth in the deep archive; the thirteenth never written, and the blank book that *would have been* it is itself an artifact: a page any truth about the Un-Signed can be written into, once, by a witness. **Quest seeds:** the folio-hunt across all seven regions; the moral fork at folio eleven; the thirteenth book, and who gets to hold the pen.

> **Design note.** Each legend seeds a quest-chain, a unique artifact with a mechanical hook, and at least one faction collision. All artifacts obey §14: none may contain, reveal, or require a god's deep name.

## 14. Canon Rules

This section is the constitution. Everything above can be extended; nothing above can be contradicted.

### 14.1 Naming rules (hard requirements)

1. **Every proper noun derives from §2** — roots from the lexicon, shapes from the phonology, affixes with their stated meanings. A name that cannot be decomposed is not canon. New roots may be added only by writing them into §2.2, with a gloss, in the same change that first uses them.
2. **The glottal compound (X'Y) is gods and god-touched artifacts only.** A mortal taking one is the Lantern King's blasphemy and may recur only as a *story about blasphemy*, never as flavor.
3. **The root `ren` is forbidden** — reserved, unglossed, against a future need. If you think you need it, you are writing the endgame; stop and convene.
4. **No god's deep name is ever written** — not in lore text, item flavor, or puzzle answers. Content that reveals one breaks the Hush, the Fray-containment, and the fiction's warranty in one stroke. Artifacts may *seek*, *approach*, or *cost* deep names; none may contain or grant one.
5. **Real-world mythological names are prohibited**, including respellings and soundalikes. The world runs on transmuted *structures*; surface names are always original Vessic.

### 14.2 The immutables (canon that may never change)

- **The three Laws (§1).** Order is rent; nothing exists unnamed; every monster has an ancestor in heaven.
- **The Hush holds.** No god manifests bodily, on screen, ever. A quest may *approach* a god asymptotically — a thread, a voice through a temple wall, a verdict — but presence is forbidden. This is the setting's deepest promise: gods absent but omnipresent.
- **Kadrash's motive stands as written.** The Severing was arithmetic and love, not jealousy. No retcon may villainize or exonerate him; the double verdict ("needful and unforgivable, and both stand") is load-bearing for the whole moral tone.
- **The Debt never clears.** No content may pay it off, cancel it, or transfer it whole.
- **The Ember is a lamp, not a god.** Khelsurat is dead. The Emberites' central error is permanent — sincere, defensible, and wrong. No content may vindicate the Khelat's theology, only its grievances.
- **Ithves cannot be killed**, only held; the Fray cannot be defeated, only fought to a standstill. Victory in Suvareth is always maintenance.
- **The dead have legal standing.** All death content routes through the corridor, the court, and the strand-anatomy of §5. No alternate afterlives.
- **The Weighing's ambiguity is permanent.** Whether the court measures the life or the preparation is never resolved; both readings stay mechanically viable forever.
- **The three creation accounts are all true.** No content may adjudicate between Loom, Drum, and Flood.
- **True permadeath is a verdict, never an accident.** The Maw acts only in story content with player-legible cause.

### 14.3 The deliberately unrevealed (mysteries under lock)

Questions, not gaps — they generate content precisely because they stay open. Answering any requires a full canon revision, which is to say: don't.

- **What Nakhoro last said** before carrying the deep names beyond the rim — "kept, not forgotten."
- **Whether the First Speaker will speak again**, and whether the Rolls of Names are, as one Registry heresy whispers, *his return being written from the inside*.
- **What the Lantern King's Last Letter says.** If ever read aloud in content, the reading happens off screen and the witnesses disagree.
- **Whether dragons are Khelsurat's heirs or his debris**; whether the First Flame is rejuvenating in death is never confirmed or denied.
- **Whether Ovet's aspect can be knotted back out of Ulveth** — the cults that dream of re-opening death's door never receive a definitive yes or no; this is the horizon the endgame walks toward without arriving.
- **What Havir actually is** — his origin is folklore, not physics; he has no entry in any Roll. Leave it that way.
- **What is on the thirteenth Nakhotess folio's blank page** until a live story event writes it, once, before witnesses.
- **What lies beyond the rim** — by definition, nothing sayable. Rim content describes the gradient, never the far side.
- **Whether the Marrowless are a wound or an adaptation** — damage, or the Weave learning. Unresolved.

### 14.4 Extension protocol

New content enters canon by attachment, never parallel invention: a new creature joins a family or founds one *with a stated origin in §3–§6*; a new region attaches to the seven with a stated stratum; a new faction states its relation to the temple economy and one existing faction; a new god may not be created — but a new *compound* may, by Zolvah's rite, in-fiction and on the page simultaneously (the Shuttered Lantern is the template). Dates use AS. The present may advance; it may never rewind. And every addition should answer the founding question this bible asks of all things, from gods to door-hinges: *who keeps it, and what does the keeping cost?*

*End of the First Canon Edition. The world holds because someone holds it. As of this page, that someone is you.*
